## Assessment 1
**Assessment number:** 1
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Overview Statements with "The chart shows/illustrates"

**Brief meaning:**
• **the chart shows**
• **the chart illustrates**
• **the chart depicts**
• **the data relates to**
• **over the period shown**

**Pre-listening:** "In IELTS Task 1, your opening sentence must paraphrase the question and describe what the visual shows. Using varied opening phrases prevents repetition and demonstrates lexical resource."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers repeat the exact wording from the question. Band 7+ writers paraphrase using synonyms like "illustrates," "depicts," and "displays" instead of always using "shows."

**Band 5-6:** "The graph shows the number of students. The graph shows the period from 2010 to 2020."
**Band 7+:** "The chart illustrates the number of students over the period shown, from 2010 to 2020."

Notice how Band 7+ combines information and varies vocabulary naturally.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Line graph about population
Band 5-6: "The graph shows population growth."
Band 7+: "The chart depicts population growth trends over the period shown."

**Situation 2:** Bar chart about sales
Band 5-6: "The chart shows sales data."
Band 7+: "The diagram illustrates sales figures across different regions."

**Situation 3:** Table about energy consumption
Band 5-6: "The table shows energy use."
Band 7+: "The data relates to energy consumption patterns between 2000 and 2020."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "shows" repeatedly ✗
• Copying the question word-for-word ✗
• Starting every sentence with "The graph shows" ✗

**Use:**
• Vary between shows/illustrates/depicts/displays ✓
• Combine information naturally ✓
• Use "over the period shown" for time references ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform this repetitive description using the lexical chunks: "The graph shows smartphone sales. It shows the years 2010 to 2020." → "The chart illustrates smartphone sales over the period shown, from 2010 to 2020."

## Assessment 2
**Assessment number:** 2
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Overall Statements - "Overall, it is clear/evident that"

**Brief meaning:**
• **overall, it is clear that**
• **overall, it is evident that**
• **overall, it can be seen that**
• **it is immediately apparent that**

**Pre-listening:** "The overview statement is MANDATORY for Band 7+. You must include 1-2 sentences summarizing the main trends BEFORE describing details. This is the most important requirement in IELTS Task 1."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers jump straight into details without an overview. Band 7+ writers ALWAYS provide an overview statement highlighting the main features before any details.

**Band 5-6:** "In 2010, sales were 100 million. In 2015, they increased to 200 million..." (No overview - jumps to details)
**Band 7+:** "Overall, it is clear that sales increased significantly throughout the period. In 2010..." (Overview first, then details)

The overview must come in the introduction or as a separate paragraph - NEVER at the end.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Graph showing rising temperatures
Band 5-6: "In 2000, the temperature was 15 degrees..."
Band 7+: "Overall, it is evident that temperatures rose consistently over the two-decade period."

**Situation 2:** Chart comparing urban vs rural population
Band 5-6: "Urban population was 60% in 1990..."
Band 7+: "It is immediately apparent that urban areas grew while rural populations declined throughout the timeframe."

**Situation 3:** Table about energy sources
Band 5-6: "Coal provided 40% of energy in 2000..."
Band 7+: "Overall, it can be seen that renewable energy sources increased significantly while fossil fuels decreased."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Starting with specific numbers/details ✗
• Putting the overview at the end ✗
• Forgetting the overview completely ✗

**Use:**
• Write overview in introduction or as paragraph 2 ✓
• Identify 1-2 main trends before details ✓
• Use "overall, it is clear that" + main trend ✓

**Recording Task:**
Add an overview statement before these details: "In 2010, renewable energy was 10%. By 2020, it reached 35%..." → "Overall, it is evident that renewable energy usage increased substantially over the decade. In 2010..."

## Assessment 3
**Assessment number:** 3
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Time Frame References - "from X to Y" and "between X and Y"

**Brief meaning:**
• **from 2010 to 2020**
• **between 2010 and 2020**
• **during the period**
• **over the period shown**
• **throughout the timeframe**

**Pre-listening:** "Accurate time references are essential in Task 1. Using varied time expressions instead of repeating dates shows lexical range and makes your writing flow naturally."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers repeat "from 2010 to 2020" multiple times. Band 7+ writers vary expressions: "during the period," "over the timeframe shown," "throughout the decade."

**Band 5-6:** "From 2010 to 2020, sales increased. From 2010 to 2020, costs also rose."
**Band 7+:** "Between 2010 and 2020, sales increased. During this period, costs also rose significantly."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Describing continuous change
Band 5-6: "From 1990 to 2020, temperatures increased."
Band 7+: "Throughout the three-decade period, temperatures increased steadily."

**Situation 2:** Multiple time references
Band 5-6: "From 2005 to 2010, exports grew. From 2010 to 2015, they declined."
Band 7+: "Between 2005 and 2010, exports grew. Over the following five years, they declined."

**Situation 3:** Referring back to the time period
Band 5-6: "From 2000 to 2020 showed interesting patterns."
Band 7+: "The data covers the period from 2000 to 2020. During this timeframe, several interesting patterns emerged."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Repeating "from X to Y" in every sentence ✗
• Omitting time references completely ✗
• Using vague terms like "recently" or "nowadays" ✗

**Use:**
• Vary between "from/to," "between/and," "during," "throughout" ✓
• Reference "the period," "the timeframe," "this duration" ✓
• Be specific with actual dates from the visual ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform this repetitive text: "From 2000 to 2010, population grew. From 2010 to 2020, it continued growing." → "Between 2000 and 2010, population grew steadily. Over the following decade, it continued this upward trend."

## Assessment 4
**Assessment number:** 4
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Comparison Structures - "while X increased, Y decreased"

**Brief meaning:**
• **while X increased, Y decreased**
• **in contrast to**
• **the most striking feature**
• **dominated throughout**
• **remained relatively stable**

**Pre-listening:** "IELTS Task 1 requires comparing data, not just describing it. Using contrast structures shows you can analyze relationships between different data sets."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers describe each data set separately: "A increased. B decreased." Band 7+ writers show relationships: "While A increased, B decreased significantly."

**Band 5-6:** "Online sales increased to 60%. Physical store sales decreased to 40%."
**Band 7+:** "While online sales increased to 60%, physical store sales decreased proportionally to 40%."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Comparing two trends
Band 5-6:** "Urban population grew. Rural population fell."
Band 7+: "While urban population grew consistently, rural population fell throughout the period."

**Situation 2:** Highlighting dominant feature
Band 5-6: "Technology sector was biggest."
Band 7+: "The most striking feature is that the technology sector dominated throughout the entire period."

**Situation 3:** Showing stability vs change
Band 5-6: "Oil consumption was stable. Solar energy increased."
Band 7+: "In contrast to oil consumption, which remained relatively stable, solar energy usage increased dramatically."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Describing each element separately without comparing ✗
• Missing obvious contrasts in the data ✗
• Listing all numbers without showing relationships ✗

**Use:**
• Use "while" to show simultaneous contrasting trends ✓
• Highlight the "most striking feature" in your overview ✓
• Use "dominated" for clearly largest categories ✓
• Use "remained stable/constant" for unchanged data ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform these separate statements into one comparison: "Asia's exports increased to $500B. Europe's exports decreased to $300B." → "While Asia's exports increased substantially to $500B, Europe's exports decreased to $300B over the same period."

## Assessment 5
**Assessment number:** 5
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Setting Context - "according to the graph/data"

**Brief meaning:**
• **according to the graph**
• **according to the data**
• **as can be seen**
• **it is noticeable that**
• **it is apparent that**

**Pre-listening:** "These phrases signal that you're referring to the visual data, not your opinion. They add formality and show you're analyzing what's shown, not speculating."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers state facts without referencing the source: "Sales increased." Band 7+ writers attribute information: "According to the graph, sales increased significantly."

**Band 5-6:** "Unemployment was 8% in 2010."
**Band 7+:** "As can be seen from the chart, unemployment stood at 8% in 2010."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Introducing data
Band 5-6: "Profits rose steadily."
Band 7+: "According to the data, profits rose steadily throughout the five-year period."

**Situation 2:** Highlighting observable patterns
Band 5-6: "Summer months had higher sales."
Band 7+: "It is noticeable that summer months consistently recorded higher sales figures."

**Situation 3:** Stating clear patterns
Band 5-6: "Younger demographics preferred online shopping."
Band 7+: "It is apparent that younger demographics showed a stronger preference for online shopping channels."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Making statements without referencing the visual ✗
• Overusing the same phrase (e.g., only "according to the graph") ✗
• Using these phrases in every single sentence ✗

**Use:**
• Vary between "according to," "as can be seen," "it is noticeable" ✓
• Use at the start of paragraphs or when introducing new information ✓
• Reserve "it is apparent/noticeable" for clear, obvious patterns ✓

**Recording Task:**
Add appropriate referencing phrases to this statement: "Sales peaked in December at $2 million. This pattern occurred every year." → "According to the data, sales peaked in December at $2 million. It is noticeable that this pattern occurred consistently every year."

## Assessment 6
**Assessment number:** 6
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Gradual Upward Trends - "rose/increased/climbed/grew"

**Brief meaning:**
• **rose**
• **increased**
• **climbed**
• **grew**
• **experienced an upward trend**

**Pre-listening:** "In IELTS Task 1, you must describe changes accurately. These basic upward movement verbs are essential and interchangeable, allowing you to avoid repetition while maintaining accuracy."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers repeat "increased" throughout their response. Band 7+ writers vary vocabulary naturally: rose, climbed, grew, experienced growth.

**Band 5-6:** "Sales increased in Q1. Profits increased in Q2. Revenue increased in Q3."
**Band 7+:** "Sales rose in Q1, while profits climbed in Q2. Subsequently, revenue grew in Q3."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Describing population change
Band 5-6: "The population increased from 5 million to 8 million."
Band 7+: "The population grew from 5 million to 8 million over the decade."

**Situation 2:** Multiple upward trends
Band 5-6: "Temperatures increased. Rainfall increased too."
Band 7+: "Temperatures rose steadily, while rainfall also climbed throughout the period."

**Situation 3:** Using the phrase form
Band 5-6: "The economy got better."
Band 7+: "The economy experienced an upward trend, with GDP climbing consistently."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using only "increased" or "went up" ✗
• Informal phrases like "got higher" or "went up" ✗
• Mixing tenses (rose/rises/has risen) incorrectly ✗

**Use:**
• Vary between rose/increased/climbed/grew ✓
• Use past simple for completed actions (rose, increased) ✓
• Add adverbs for more precision (rose steadily, increased sharply) ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform this repetitive description: "Exports increased in 2010. They increased again in 2015. They increased more in 2020." → "Exports rose in 2010, climbed further in 2015, and grew substantially by 2020."

## Assessment 7
**Assessment number:** 7
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Slow/Steady Increases - "crept up gradually"

**Brief meaning:**
• **crept up gradually**
• **increased steadily**
• **rose gradually**
• **grew slowly but steadily**

**Pre-listening:** "Not all increases are the same. Band 7+ writers distinguish between gradual, steady increases and sharp rises. The adverb + verb combination shows precise understanding of the data."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers use basic verbs without adverbs: "increased." Band 7+ writers add descriptive adverbs: "increased steadily," "crept up gradually."

**Band 5-6:** "House prices increased from 2010 to 2020."
**Band 7+:** "House prices crept up gradually from 2010 to 2020, showing consistent but modest growth."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Describing slow, consistent growth
Band 5-6: "Inflation increased slowly."
Band 7+: "Inflation rose gradually, creeping up by approximately 0.5% annually."

**Situation 2:** Contrasting fast and slow growth
Band 5-6: "Technology jobs increased. Manufacturing jobs increased too."
Band 7+: "While technology jobs surged, manufacturing positions increased steadily at a much slower pace."

**Situation 3:** Long-term gradual change
Band 5-6: "Life expectancy increased over 50 years."
Band 7+: "Life expectancy grew slowly but steadily over the five-decade period, increasing by roughly 15 years total."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Describing all increases the same way ✗
• Missing the speed/pace of change ✗
• Using "slowly" alone without a verb ✗

**Use:**
• Match adverb to the actual pace shown in visual ✓
• Use "gradually," "steadily," "slowly" for gentle slopes ✓
• Combine adverbs: "slowly but steadily" for emphasis ✓

**Recording Task:**
Make this description more precise: "Savings rates increased between 2000 and 2020." → "Savings rates crept up gradually between 2000 and 2020, rising steadily by approximately 2 percentage points per decade."

## Assessment 8
**Assessment number:** 8
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Dramatic Increases - "surged/soared/rocketed"

**Brief meaning:**
• **surged**
• **soared**
• **rocketed**
• **increased sharply**
• **rose dramatically**

**Pre-listening:** "These verbs describe rapid, significant increases. Use them for steep upward trends in graphs. They're more powerful than 'increased a lot' and show sophisticated vocabulary."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers say "increased a lot" or "increased very much." Band 7+ writers use precise vocabulary: surged, soared, rocketed for dramatic changes.

**Band 5-6:** "Smartphone sales increased a lot in 2015."
**Band 7+:** "Smartphone sales soared in 2015, increasing by over 200% in a single year."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Describing sudden, sharp increase
Band 5-6: "Oil prices increased very quickly."
Band 7+: "Oil prices surged dramatically, rocketing from $50 to $120 per barrel within six months."

**Situation 2:** Comparing normal vs dramatic growth
Band 5-6: "Sales increased slowly then increased fast."
Band 7+: "Sales crept up gradually until 2018, when they suddenly soared, doubling within two years."

**Situation 3:** Multiple dramatic increases
Band 5-6: "Electric vehicle sales increased a lot. Battery production increased a lot too."
Band 7+: "Electric vehicle sales soared throughout the period, while battery production likewise surged to meet demand."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "increased a lot," "went up a lot" ✗
• Using dramatic verbs for small increases ✗
• Overusing the same dramatic verb repeatedly ✗

**Use:**
• Reserve surged/soared/rocketed for truly steep increases ✓
• Vary between these three verbs ✓
• Support with data: "soared by 150%" or "rocketed to $2M" ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform this weak description: "Bitcoin prices increased a lot in 2017." → "Bitcoin prices rocketed in 2017, soaring from approximately $1,000 to nearly $20,000 by year's end."

## Assessment 9
**Assessment number:** 9
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Peak/Maximum Points - "peaked at"

**Brief meaning:**
• **peaked at**
• **reached a peak of**
• **hit a maximum of**
• **topped out at**

**Pre-listening:** "Identifying and describing peak points is crucial in Task 1. These phrases help you highlight the highest point in a trend clearly and precisely."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers say "was highest at" or "the maximum was." Band 7+ writers use "peaked at" or "reached a peak of" for natural, precise expression.

**Band 5-6:** "Sales were highest in December. The highest number was 500,000."
**Band 7+:** "Sales peaked in December, reaching a maximum of 500,000 units."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Identifying the highest point
Band 5-6: "Temperatures were highest in July at 35 degrees."
Band 7+: "Temperatures peaked in July at 35 degrees before declining in August."

**Situation 2:** Describing when peak occurred
Band 5-6: "Unemployment was highest in 2009."
Band 7+: "Unemployment reached a peak of 10% in 2009, during the financial crisis."

**Situation 3:** Multiple peaks
Band 5-6: "Traffic was highest at 8am and 5pm."
Band 7+: "Traffic volumes peaked twice daily, topping out at 8am and 5pm during rush hours."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Saying "was highest" or "was maximum" ✗
• Forgetting to mention peak values ✗
• Confusing "peak" (highest) with "peak" (verb) ✗

**Use:**
• "Peaked at" + specific number/value ✓
• "Reached a peak of" for formal emphasis ✓
• Mention when the peak occurred (year/month/period) ✓

**Recording Task:**
Improve this description: "Energy consumption was highest in winter. It was 500 TWh." → "Energy consumption peaked during winter months, reaching a maximum of 500 TWh in January."

## Assessment 10
**Assessment number:** 10
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Pattern Recognition - "experienced an upward trend"

**Brief meaning:**
• **experienced an upward trend**
• **showed an increasing pattern**
• **displayed a rising tendency**
• **demonstrated consistent growth**

**Pre-listening:** "Instead of just saying 'increased,' Band 7+ writers describe patterns and trends. These phrases show you're analyzing the overall movement, not just individual data points."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers describe individual changes: "went up, then up, then up." Band 7+ writers identify patterns: "experienced an upward trend throughout the period."

**Band 5-6:** "In 2010 sales increased. In 2015 they increased. In 2020 they increased."
**Band 7+:** "Sales experienced a consistent upward trend throughout the decade, increasing in every measured period."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Describing overall pattern
Band 5-6: "Internet users went up every year."
Band 7+: "Internet usage demonstrated consistent growth, with user numbers increasing annually throughout the timeframe."

**Situation 2:** Comparing patterns
Band 5-6: "Renewable energy went up. Fossil fuels went down."
Band 7+: "While renewable energy experienced an upward trend, fossil fuel consumption displayed the opposite pattern."

**Situation 3:** Noting trend despite fluctuations
Band 5-6: "GDP increased mostly, with some small decreases."
Band 7+: "Despite minor fluctuations, GDP showed an overall increasing pattern, trending upward across the decade."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Describing every single data point separately ✗
• Missing the overall pattern/trend ✗
• Using "trend" incorrectly (it should describe overall movement) ✗

**Use:**
• Use "trend" to describe overall direction ✓
• "Experienced/showed/displayed" + trend pattern ✓
• Support trend observations with specific evidence ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform these separate observations into a trend statement: "In 2010, mobile phone users increased. In 2012, they increased. In 2015, they increased. In 2020, they increased." → "Mobile phone users experienced a consistent upward trend throughout the decade, increasing steadily across all measured periods."
