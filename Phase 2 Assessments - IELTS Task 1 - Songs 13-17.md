## Assessment 51
**Assessment number:** 51
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Expressing Purpose - "in order to / so as to / to"

**Brief meaning:**
• **in order to**
• **so as to**
• **to** (purpose)
• **for the purpose of**
• **with the aim of**

**Pre-listening:** "Process descriptions should explain WHY steps occur. Band 7+ writers use purpose expressions to show the reason behind each action, making descriptions more analytical."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers only describe what happens: "Water is heated." Band 7+ writers explain why: "Water is heated in order to kill bacteria."

**Band 5-6:** "The mixture is stirred for 10 minutes."
**Band 7+:** "The mixture is stirred continuously for 10 minutes in order to ensure even distribution of ingredients."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Manufacturing purpose
Band 5-6: "Materials are inspected."
Band 7+: "Materials are inspected to identify any defects before processing begins."

**Situation 2:** Treatment purpose
Band 5-6: "Milk is pasteurized at 72°C."
Band 7+: "Milk is pasteurized at 72°C so as to eliminate harmful bacteria while preserving nutritional content."

**Situation 3:** Formal purpose expression
Band 5-6: "Products are packaged in sealed containers."
Band 7+: "Products are packaged in sealed containers with the aim of maintaining freshness and preventing contamination."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "for + -ing" (incorrect: "for removing") ✗
• Missing purpose clauses entirely ✗
• Adding obvious purposes that add no value ✗

**Use:**
• "to + base verb" for simple purpose ✓
• "in order to / so as to" for formal emphasis ✓
• "with the aim of / for the purpose of" + -ing for formal style ✓

**Recording Task:**
Add purpose clauses: "Ingredients are weighed precisely. Temperature is monitored throughout. Products are sealed immediately after filling." → "Ingredients are weighed precisely in order to maintain consistency across batches. Temperature is monitored continuously to ensure optimal conditions. Products are sealed immediately after filling so as to preserve freshness."

## Assessment 52
**Assessment number:** 52
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Expressing Result - "resulting in / leading to / causing"

**Brief meaning:**
• **resulting in**
• **leading to**
• **causing**
• **which results in**
• **which leads to**

**Pre-listening:** "While purpose shows intention, result shows consequence. Band 7+ writers distinguish between why something is done (purpose) and what happens as a consequence (result)."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers don't show cause-and-effect relationships. Band 7+ writers connect actions to outcomes: "which results in," "leading to."

**Band 5-6:** "The mixture is heated. It becomes thick."
**Band 7+:** "The mixture is heated to 90°C, resulting in a thick, paste-like consistency."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Manufacturing outcome
Band 5-6: "Metal is cooled quickly. It becomes hard."
Band 7+: "Metal is cooled rapidly, leading to increased hardness and durability."

**Situation 2:** Chemical result
Band 5-6: "Chemicals are added. The pH changes."
Band 7+: "Chemical additives are introduced, causing the pH to stabilize at approximately 7.0."

**Situation 3:** Process consequence
Band 5-6: "Pressure is increased. Molecules bond together."
Band 7+: "Pressure is increased to 1000 bar, which results in molecular bonding and material transformation."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Confusing purpose (intention) with result (outcome) ✗
• Using "causing" for everything (vary with "resulting in," "leading to") ✗
• Missing clear cause-effect relationships in the data ✗

**Use:**
• "resulting in" for direct outcomes ✓
• "leading to" for consequences ✓
• "which results in / which leads to" for relative clauses ✓
• "causing" for clear causation ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform into result clauses: "Flour is sifted. Air is incorporated. The texture becomes lighter." → "Flour is sifted multiple times, which results in air incorporation and leads to a lighter, more refined texture."

## Assessment 53
**Assessment number:** 53
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Enabling/Allowing - "which allows / which enables / which permits"

**Brief meaning:**
• **which allows**
• **which enables**
• **which permits**
• **allowing**
• **enabling**

**Pre-listening:** "These expressions show that one step makes another step possible. Band 7+ writers use them to show how processes enable subsequent actions."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers describe steps independently. Band 7+ writers show how steps enable each other: "which allows," "enabling."

**Band 5-6:** "Water is filtered. Then it can be bottled."
**Band 7+:** "Water is filtered through multiple stages, which allows it to meet safety standards for bottling."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Process enablement
Band 5-6: "Materials are dried. Then they can be stored."
Band 7+: "Materials are thoroughly dried, which enables long-term storage without deterioration."

**Situation 2:** Gerund form
Band 5-6: "The design has openings. Air can circulate."
Band 7+: "The design incorporates ventilation openings, allowing air to circulate freely and prevent moisture buildup."

**Situation 3:** Permission/capability
Band 5-6: "Products are coded. They can be tracked."
Band 7+: "Products are assigned unique QR codes, which permits tracking throughout the entire supply chain."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "which makes" instead of "which allows" ✗
• Confusing "allows to" (wrong) with "allows X to" (correct) ✗
• Overusing "which allows" in every sentence ✗

**Use:**
• "which allows/enables/permits" + object + to + verb ✓
• "allowing/enabling" + object + to + verb (gerund form) ✓
• Show what becomes possible as a result ✓

**Recording Task:**
Use allowing/enabling structures: "Automated systems sort items by size. Workers can focus on quality control. This increases efficiency." → "Automated systems sort items by size, allowing workers to focus on quality control and enabling the facility to increase overall efficiency by 30%."

## Assessment 54
**Assessment number:** 54
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Purpose with "so that / in order that"

**Brief meaning:**
• **so that**
• **in order that**
• **such that**

**Pre-listening:** "When purpose involves a clause (subject + verb), use 'so that' or 'in order that' instead of just 'to.' Band 7+ writers use these correctly in formal process descriptions."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers might use incorrect structures. Band 7+ writers choose correctly: "to + verb" vs "so that + clause."

**Band 5-6:** "Materials are labeled to workers can identify them." (Incorrect structure)
**Band 7+:** "Materials are labeled so that workers can quickly identify and select the correct components."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Worker action purpose
Band 5-6: "Temperature is displayed to operators can adjust it."
Band 7+: "Temperature is displayed on digital monitors so that operators can make real-time adjustments as needed."

**Situation 2:** Quality assurance purpose
Band 5-6: "Each stage is documented to inspectors can verify."
Band 7+: "Each stage is thoroughly documented in order that quality inspectors can verify compliance with safety standards."

**Situation 3:** System design purpose
Band 5-6: "The system has automatic shutoff to accidents can be prevented."
Band 7+: "The system incorporates automatic shutoff mechanisms so that potential accidents can be prevented immediately."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "to" when you need a clause ("to they can") ✗
• Forgetting "that" after "so" in formal writing ✗
• Mixing up "so that" (purpose) with "so" (result) ✗

**Use:**
• "so that" + subject + can/will/may + verb ✓
• "in order that" for very formal contexts ✓
• Use when purpose involves someone/something doing an action ✓

**Recording Task:**
Correct the structures: "Products are color-coded to customers can distinguish types. Alarms are installed to staff can respond quickly." → "Products are color-coded so that customers can easily distinguish between different types. Alarms are installed in order that staff can respond quickly to any malfunctions."

## Assessment 55
**Assessment number:** 55
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Combining Purpose and Result

**Brief meaning:**
• **Purpose → Result chain**
• **in order to X, resulting in Y**
• **to X, which leads to Y**

**Pre-listening:** "Band 7+ process descriptions often combine purpose and result to show complete cause-effect chains: an action is done for a purpose, which results in an outcome."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers describe isolated steps. Band 7+ writers show chains: "X is done to achieve Y, resulting in Z."

**Band 5-6:** "Materials are sorted. They are cleaned. Quality improves."
**Band 7+:** "Materials are sorted by type in order to optimize cleaning procedures, resulting in improved overall product quality."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Complete chain
Band 5-6: "Water is filtered. Impurities are removed. Water becomes safe."
Band 7+: "Water is filtered through activated carbon to remove chemical impurities, resulting in safe drinking water that meets WHO standards."

**Situation 2:** Manufacturing chain
Band 5-6: "Metal is heated. It becomes malleable. Shaping is easier."
Band 7+: "Metal is heated to 800°C in order to increase malleability, which allows for easier shaping and leads to more precise final products."

**Situation 3:** Multiple results
Band 5-6: "Packaging is vacuum-sealed. Air is removed. Freshness is maintained. Shelf life increases."
Band 7+: "Packaging is vacuum-sealed to remove oxygen, preventing oxidation and resulting in extended shelf life of up to 12 months."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Creating overly complex chains that confuse readers ✗
• Missing the logical connections between steps ✗
• Using "because" in process descriptions (use purpose/result instead) ✗

**Use:**
• Purpose + result: "to X, resulting in Y" ✓
• Purpose + enablement + result: "to X, which allows Y, leading to Z" ✓
• Keep chains logical and clear ✓

**Recording Task:**
Create purpose-result chains: "Cocoa is fermented. Flavor develops. Chocolate tastes better. Beans are dried. Moisture is removed. Mold is prevented." → "Cocoa beans are fermented for 5-7 days to develop complex flavor compounds, resulting in richer chocolate taste. Subsequently, beans are dried in the sun to remove excess moisture, which prevents mold growth and enables safe long-term storage."

## Assessment 56
**Assessment number:** 56
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Cardinal Directions - "to the north/south/east/west of"

**Brief meaning:**
• **to the north of**
• **to the south of**
• **to the east of**
• **to the west of**
• **in the northern/southern/eastern/western part**

**Pre-listening:** "Map descriptions require precise location language. Band 7+ writers use cardinal directions to describe where features are located relative to each other."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers use vague locations: "at the top," "on the side." Band 7+ writers use precise directions: "to the north of," "in the eastern part."

**Band 5-6:** "The park is at the top of the map."
**Band 7+:** "The park is situated to the north of the town center."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Describing relative positions
Band 5-6: "The hospital is above the school."
Band 7+: "The hospital is located to the north of the school, approximately 500 meters away."

**Situation 2:** Regional descriptions
Band 5-6: "Farms are on the right side of the area."
Band 7+: "Agricultural land is concentrated in the eastern part of the region."

**Situation 3:** Multiple directional references
Band 5-6: "The river goes across the map from left to right."
Band 7+: "The river flows from the western boundary to the eastern edge, bisecting the town."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "above/below" (use north/south) ✗
• Using "left/right" (use west/east) ✗
• Mixing up directions ✗

**Use:**
• "to the north/south/east/west of" for relative position ✓
• "in the northern/southern/eastern/western part" for regions ✓
• Can combine: "in the northeastern corner" ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform using cardinal directions: "The factory is at the bottom left. The residential area is at the top right." → "The factory is located in the southwestern corner of the development, while the residential area occupies the northeastern section."

## Assessment 57
**Assessment number:** 57
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Position Verbs - "situated / located / positioned"

**Brief meaning:**
• **is situated**
• **is located**
• **is positioned**
• **stands**
• **lies**

**Pre-listening:** "These verbs describe where things are placed. Band 7+ writers vary between situated, located, and positioned instead of repeating 'is' for every feature."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers use only "is": "The building is in the center." Band 7+ writers vary: "is situated," "is located," "stands."

**Band 5-6:** "The library is in the middle of the town."
**Band 7+:** "The library is situated in the town center, adjacent to the main square."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Formal positioning
Band 5-6: "The monument is in the park."
Band 7+: "The monument is positioned at the center of the park, surrounded by landscaped gardens."

**Situation 2:** Building location
Band 5-6: "The office building is near the station."
Band 7+: "The office complex is located approximately 200 meters east of the railway station."

**Situation 3:** Natural features
Band 5-6: "The lake is at the edge."
Band 7+: "The lake lies along the western boundary of the conservation area."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using only "is" for all locations ✗
• Saying "is situating" (incorrect - use "is situated") ✗
• Using "located at" for areas (use "located in" for areas, "at" for points) ✗

**Use:**
• "Is situated/located" most common and versatile ✓
• "Is positioned" for deliberate placement ✓
• "Stands" for buildings/structures ✓
• "Lies" for geographical features ✓

**Recording Task:**
Vary the position verbs: "The school is in the north. The hospital is near it. The park is between them." → "The school is situated in the northern district. The hospital stands adjacent to it, approximately 300 meters to the east. A recreational park is positioned between these two facilities."

• **adjacent to**
• **next to**
• **beside**
• **near**
• **in the vicinity of**

## Assessment 60
**Assessment number:** 60
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Combining All Location Language

**Brief meaning:**
• **Complete spatial descriptions**
• **Cardinal directions + position + proximity**
• **Creating clear mental maps**

**Pre-listening:** "Band 7+ map descriptions integrate all location language: directions (north/south), position (situated/located), and proximity (adjacent/opposite) to create comprehensive spatial descriptions."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers give disconnected locations. Band 7+ writers create integrated descriptions showing all spatial relationships.

**Band 5-6:** "There is a park. There is a school. There is a hospital. They are in different places."

**Band 7+:** "A large public park is situated in the northern part of the town. Immediately adjacent to its eastern boundary stands the primary school. The hospital is located approximately 500 meters to the south of the park, positioned opposite the town center across Main Street."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Complete town layout description:**

Band 5-6: "The shopping center is at the top. Roads are in the middle. Houses are at the bottom and on the sides. A river is on the left."

Band 7+: "The shopping complex is situated in the northern district of the town, adjacent to the highway interchange. The central area is occupied by a network of roads connecting various zones. Residential neighborhoods are positioned in the southern and eastern sections, while a river runs along the entire western boundary of the development. Industrial facilities are located to the east, in close proximity to the main transport routes."

**Recording Tips:**

**Use integrated spatial language:**
• Start with cardinal direction ✓
• Add position verb (situated, located) ✓
• Include proximity relationships ✓
• Use opposite/facing for relationships across space ✓
• Create logical flow from one feature to another ✓

**Recording Task:**
Write a complete spatial description: "Describe the layout of a university campus that has: a library in the center, lecture halls to the north, student residences to the south, sports facilities to the east, and a park to the west. A main road runs east-west through the middle."

Band 7+ version: "The campus is centered around the main library, which is strategically positioned at the heart of the development. To the north of the library lie the lecture halls and academic buildings, arranged in a semicircular formation. Student accommodation is situated in the southern section, approximately 300 meters from the central facilities. Sports complexes, including a gymnasium and playing fields, are located in the eastern part of the campus. A landscaped park occupies the western zone, providing green space adjacent to the library. A main thoroughfare runs east-west through the campus, bisecting it and providing access to all major facilities. The northern and southern zones face each other across this central road."

## Assessment 61
**Assessment number:** 61
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Removal/Demolition - "was replaced by / was demolished / was removed"

**Brief meaning:**
• **was replaced by**
• **was demolished**
• **was removed**
• **was knocked down**
• **gave way to**

**Pre-listening:** "Map transformation descriptions show what disappeared. Band 7+ writers use precise verbs for removal: demolished (buildings), removed (features), replaced by (substitution)."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers say "is not there anymore." Band 7+ writers use specific verbs: "was demolished," "was replaced by."

**Band 5-6:** "The old factory is not there now. There is a shopping mall instead."
**Band 7+:** "The old factory was demolished and replaced by a modern shopping mall."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Complete replacement
Band 5-6: "Farmland is gone. Houses are there now."
Band 7+: "Agricultural land was replaced by residential housing developments between 2000 and 2020."

**Situation 2:** Demolition
Band 5-6: "The warehouse is not on the 2020 map."
Band 7+: "The warehouse that stood on the site was demolished in 2015 to make way for new development."

**Situation 3:** Feature removal
Band 5-6: "Trees are not there anymore."
Band 7+: "The wooded area was removed to accommodate the expansion of the parking facilities."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "disappeared" or "is gone" ✗
• Saying "was replaced with" (use "was replaced by") ✗
• Missing the time period when changes occurred ✗

**Use:**
• "Was demolished" for structures ✓
• "Was removed" for features/vegetation ✓
• "Was replaced by" + what took its place ✓
• "Gave way to" for formal writing ✓

**Recording Task:**
Use transformation verbs: "In 2000, there was a church in the center. In 2020, there is a community center." → "The historic church that occupied the central location was demolished in 2010 and replaced by a modern community center, which opened in 2012."
