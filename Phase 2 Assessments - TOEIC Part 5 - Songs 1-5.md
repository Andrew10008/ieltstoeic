## Assessment 1
**Assessment number:** 1
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Subject-Verb Agreement with Separated Subjects

**Brief meaning:**
• **the company has**
• **the companies have**
• **the list of requirements is**
• **the box of documents is**

**Pre-listening:** "In TOEIC Part 5, you must identify the true subject of the sentence, not the nouns that come between the subject and verb. This skill prevents 60% of subject-verb agreement errors."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
TOEIC test-takers scoring 400-500 often match verbs to the nearest noun instead of the actual subject. Those scoring 650+ identify the true subject by locating it before prepositional phrases.

**Score 400-500:** "The list of documents _are_ on the desk." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The list of documents _is_ on the desk." ✓

The subject is "list" (singular), not "documents" (which is part of the prepositional phrase). Singular subject = singular verb.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Business report context
Score 400-500: "The results of the survey _shows_ customer satisfaction."
Score 650+: "The results of the survey _show_ customer satisfaction."
(Subject: "results" = plural)

**Situation 2:** Meeting notice
Score 400-500: "The manager, along with the team members, _are_ attending."
Score 650+: "The manager, along with the team members, _is_ attending."
(Subject: "manager" = singular; "along with..." is additional information)

**Situation 3:** Financial statement
Score 400-500: "The box of chocolates and flowers _were_ delivered."
Score 650+: "The box of chocolates and flowers _was_ delivered."
(Subject: "box" = singular)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Looking at the noun closest to the blank ✗
• Assuming plural-looking phrases need plural verbs ✗
• Rushing without identifying the subject ✗

**Use:**
• Cross out prepositional phrases (of, with, along with, in, on, etc.) ✓
• Find the subject before the phrase ✓
• Ask "Who or what is doing the action?" ✓
• Check if subject is singular or plural ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform this incorrect sentence using the lexical chunks: "The company's list of new employees _are_ being processed." → "The company's list of new employees _is_ being processed."

## Assessment 2
**Assessment number:** 2
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Indefinite Pronouns (Each, Every, Everyone, etc.)

**Brief meaning:**
• **each employee needs**
• **all employees need**
• **everyone has**
• **many have**

**Pre-listening:** "Indefinite pronouns like 'each,' 'every,' 'everyone,' and 'everybody' are ALWAYS singular in English, even though they refer to multiple people. This is a high-frequency TOEIC trap."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers often think "everyone" and "everybody" are plural because they mean "all people." Higher-scoring test-takers memorize that these pronouns are grammatically singular.

**Score 400-500:** "Everyone _have_ received their login credentials." ✗
**Score 650+:** "Everyone _has_ received their login credentials." ✓

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Training requirement
Score 400-500: "Each of the candidates _have_ their own strengths."
Score 650+: "Each of the candidates _has_ their own strengths."

**Situation 2:** Office policy
Score 400-500: "Every employee _are_ required to attend the safety briefing."
Score 650+: "Every employee _is_ required to attend the safety briefing."

**Situation 3:** Department meeting
Score 400-500: "Somebody from accounting _need_ to approve this."
Score 650+: "Somebody from accounting _needs_ to approve this."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Thinking "each of the [plural]" is plural ✗
• Using plural verbs with everyone/everybody/someone/anyone ✗
• Following instinct instead of grammar rules ✗

**Use:**
• Memorize: each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, anyone, nobody = SINGULAR ✓
• Some, all, most = depends on what follows (countable/uncountable) ✓
• Practice with flashcards until automatic ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "Each of the managers _have_ submitted their reports." → "Each of the managers _has_ submitted their reports."

## Assessment 3
**Assessment number:** 3
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Compound Subjects (And, Or, Neither/Nor)

**Brief meaning:**
• **my colleague and I are**
• **one of them is**
• **several of them are**
• **neither option seems**
• **both options seem**

**Pre-listening:** "When subjects are joined by 'and,' they're usually plural. But watch for exceptions and tricky patterns with 'or' and 'neither/nor' that TOEIC loves to test."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
TOEIC tests whether you know that compound subjects joined by "and" are plural, while subjects with "or" or "neither/nor" match the verb to the NEAREST subject.

**Score 400-500:** "My colleague and I _is_ going to the conference." ✗
**Score 650+:** "My colleague and I _are_ going to the conference." ✓

Two subjects joined by "and" = plural verb.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Meeting attendance
Score 400-500: "The manager or the team members _is_ responsible."
Score 650+: "The manager or the team members _are_ responsible."
(Verb matches the NEAREST subject: "members" = plural)

**Situation 2:** Decision making
Score 400-500: "Neither the proposal nor the alternatives _seems_ acceptable."
Score 650+: "Neither the proposal nor the alternatives _seem_ acceptable."
(Match to "alternatives" = plural)

**Situation 3:** Project assignment
Score 400-500: "Both the schedule and the budget _needs_ approval."
Score 650+: "Both the schedule and the budget _need_ approval."
("Both" always takes plural)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using singular verbs with "Subject A and Subject B" ✗
• Matching verb to the first subject when using "or" ✗
• Forgetting that "both" is always plural ✗

**Use:**
• And = plural (usually) ✓
• Or / Neither...nor = match the NEAREST subject ✓
• Both = always plural ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "Neither the timeline nor the budget _allow_ for changes." → "Neither the timeline nor the budget _allows_ for changes." (Match "budget" = singular)

## Assessment 4

**Brief meaning:**
• **the team is**

• **the company has**
• **the staff is** (American) / **the staff are** (British)

**Pre-listening:** "Collective nouns in American English (TOEIC standard) are usually singular. The team IS, not the team ARE. But watch for context where individuals are emphasized."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
In American business English (TOEIC uses this standard), collective nouns like team, committee, company, and staff are treated as singular units.

**Score 400-500:** "The committee _are_ meeting on Tuesday." ✗

**Situation 2:** Department activity

(In American English; British would allow "are")

**Situation 3:** Group decision
Score 400-500: "The board of directors _have_ approved the merger."
Score 650+: "The board of directors _has_ approved the merger."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using plural verbs with team, committee, company, staff ✗
• Mixing British and American conventions ✗

**Use:**
• In TOEIC context, treat collective nouns as singular ✓
• Ask: "Is it acting as one unit?" → singular ✓
• Memorize common collectives: team, committee, company, staff, board, group ✓

**Recording Task:**

("Employees" = countable plural)

**Situation 3:** Equipment check
Score 400-500: "None of the equipment _are_ functioning properly."
Score 650+: "None of the equipment _is_ functioning properly."
("Equipment" = uncountable)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Treating all uncountable nouns as plural ✗
• Forgetting that information, equipment, furniture, advice are uncountable ✗

**Use:**
• Identify if the noun after "of" is countable or uncountable ✓
• Countable plural → plural verb ✓
• Uncountable → singular verb ✓
• Memorize common uncountables: information, equipment, furniture, advice, research ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "All of the research _indicate_ promising results." → "All of the research _indicates_ promising results."

## Assessment 6
**Assessment number:** 6
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Simple Past vs. Present Perfect

**Brief meaning:**
• **yesterday we completed**
• **last week we finished**
• **we have completed** (unspecified time)
• **since 2020 we have worked**

**Pre-listening:** "Time markers are your best friends in TOEIC Part 5. Words like 'yesterday,' 'last week,' and 'ago' require simple past, while 'since,' 'for,' and 'already' require present perfect. This distinction appears in 15-20% of Part 5 questions."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 often confuse simple past and present perfect. Those scoring 650+ use time markers to choose the correct tense automatically.

**Score 400-500:** "Yesterday, we have completed the training session." ✗
**Score 650+:** "Yesterday, we completed the training session." ✓

"Yesterday" = specific past time → simple past only.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Project completion report
Score 400-500: "The team completed the project since January."
Score 650+: "The team has completed the project since January."
("Since" = present perfect required)

**Situation 2:** Meeting record
Score 400-500: "We have met with the client last Monday."
Score 650+: "We met with the client last Monday."
("Last Monday" = specific time → simple past)

**Situation 3:** Work experience
Score 400-500: "She works here for five years."
Score 650+: "She has worked here for five years."
("For five years" continuing to now = present perfect)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "have/has" with yesterday, last week, ago ✗
• Using simple past with since, for, already, yet ✗
• Ignoring time markers in the sentence ✗

**Use:**
• Yesterday/last week/ago → simple past ✓
• Since/for/already/yet → present perfect ✓
• Circle time markers before choosing tense ✓
• No time marker → check context for relevance to now ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "We have submitted the proposal last Friday." → "We submitted the proposal last Friday."

## Assessment 7
**Assessment number:** 7
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Present Continuous vs. Simple Present

**Brief meaning:**
• **currently we are working**
• **right now she is reviewing**
• **every day we work**
• **usually we review**

**Pre-listening:** "Present continuous (am/is/are + -ing) describes actions happening NOW or temporarily. Simple present describes habits, routines, and permanent situations. TOEIC tests this distinction with time markers like 'currently,' 'at the moment,' 'every day,' and 'usually.'"

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers choose tenses randomly. Higher-scoring test-takers recognize that "currently" and "at the moment" signal present continuous, while "every day" and "usually" signal simple present.

**Score 400-500:** "Currently, the company implements new policies." ✗
**Score 650+:** "Currently, the company is implementing new policies." ✓

"Currently" indicates an ongoing temporary action → present continuous.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Office routine
Score 400-500: "The manager is holding meetings every Monday."
Score 650+: "The manager holds meetings every Monday."
("Every Monday" = routine → simple present)

**Situation 2:** Temporary project
Score 400-500: "Right now, the team works on a special assignment."
Score 650+: "Right now, the team is working on a special assignment."
("Right now" = happening now → present continuous)

**Situation 3:** Permanent arrangement
Score 400-500: "The office is opening at 9 AM daily."
Score 650+: "The office opens at 9 AM daily."
("Daily" = routine → simple present)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using simple present with currently, right now, at the moment ✗
• Using continuous with every day, usually, always, often ✗
• Confusing temporary vs. permanent situations ✗

**Use:**
• Currently/right now/at the moment → present continuous ✓
• Every day/usually/always → simple present ✓
• Ask: "Is this temporary or permanent?" ✓
• Temporary → continuous; Routine → simple ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "At the moment, we handle three major clients." → "At the moment, we are handling three major clients."

## Assessment 8
**Assessment number:** 8
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Future Tenses (Will vs. Going to vs. Future Perfect)

**Brief meaning:**
• **tomorrow we will meet**
• **we are going to launch** (planned)
• **by next month we will have completed**
• **the meeting starts at 2 PM** (scheduled)

**Pre-listening:** "English has multiple ways to express future actions. TOEIC tests whether you know that 'will' is for decisions/predictions, 'going to' is for plans/evidence, 'will have + past participle' is for completion before a future time, and simple present is for schedules."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 use "will" for everything. Those scoring 650+ recognize different future forms serve different purposes.

**Score 400-500:** "By the end of this quarter, we will complete the project." ✗
**Score 650+:** "By the end of this quarter, we will have completed the project." ✓

"By [future time]" = action will be finished before that time → future perfect.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Scheduled event
Score 400-500: "The conference will begin at 9 AM tomorrow."
Score 650+: "The conference begins at 9 AM tomorrow."
(Fixed schedule → simple present)

**Situation 2:** Existing plan
Score 400-500: "We will launch the product next month—everything is ready."
Score 650+: "We are going to launch the product next month—everything is ready."
(Prepared plan with evidence → going to)

**Situation 3:** Completion deadline
Score 400-500: "By Friday, I will finish all the reports."
Score 650+: "By Friday, I will have finished all the reports."
("By Friday" = will be done before Friday → future perfect)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "will" for scheduled events (use simple present) ✗
• Using "will" when there's evidence/plan (use going to) ✗
• Forgetting "will have + past participle" for "by [time]" ✗

**Use:**
• Scheduled events (times) → simple present ✓
• Plans/evidence → going to ✓
• Predictions/decisions → will ✓
• By [future time] → will have + past participle ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "By 5 PM today, we will send all the invitations." → "By 5 PM today, we will have sent all the invitations."

## Assessment 10
**Assessment number:** 10
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Past Perfect vs. Simple Past (Sequence of Past Events)

**Brief meaning:**
• **we had completed** (earlier past action)
• **before the meeting started, we had reviewed**
• **after we finished, we submitted**
• **when we arrived, they had already left**

**Pre-listening:** "When describing two past actions, past perfect (had + past participle) shows which happened FIRST. This is crucial in TOEIC sentences with 'before,' 'after,' 'when,' and 'by the time.' The earlier action gets past perfect."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 use simple past for everything. Those scoring 650+ use past perfect to show which past action happened first.

**Score 400-500:** "By the time we arrived, the meeting started." ✗
**Score 650+:** "By the time we arrived, the meeting had started." ✓

The meeting started BEFORE we arrived → earlier action gets past perfect.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Preparation sequence
Score 400-500: "Before we submitted the proposal, we reviewed it carefully."
Score 650+: "Before we submitted the proposal, we had reviewed it carefully."
(Reviewing happened before submitting → past perfect for earlier action)

**Situation 2:** Discovery of prior action
Score 400-500: "When I checked my email, the manager sent the schedule."
Score 650+: "When I checked my email, the manager had sent the schedule."
(Sending happened before checking → past perfect)

**Situation 3:** After sequence
Score 400-500: "After the team had finished the report, they submitted it."
Score 650+: "After the team finished the report, they submitted it."
("After" makes sequence clear, so simple past is fine—but past perfect is also correct)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using simple past for both actions when sequence matters ✗
• Overusing past perfect (sometimes simple past is clear enough) ✗
• Forgetting "had + past participle" structure ✗

**Use:**
• By the time, when, before → usually past perfect for earlier action ✓
• Look for two past actions in one sentence ✓
• Earlier action → past perfect; later action → simple past ✓
• "After" often allows simple past for both (sequence is clear) ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "When we arrived at the office, the director announced the changes." → "When we arrived at the office, the director had announced the changes."

• Using "the" for general statements ✗

**Use:**
• First mention, not specific → a/an ✓
• Second mention or specific → the ✓
• Unique in context (the CEO, the sun, the manager [if only one]) → the ✓

## Assessment 17
**Assessment number:** 17
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** "A" vs. "An" (Vowel Sound Rule)

**Brief meaning:**

• **an** (before vowel sounds: an employee, an hour, an MBA)

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers choose based on the letter. Higher-scoring test-takers choose based on the SOUND.

**Score 400-500:** "She has a MBA from Harvard." ✗
**Score 650+:** "She has an MBA from Harvard." ✓

**Practice Scenarios:**

Score 400-500: "This is an unique opportunity."
Score 650+: "This is a unique opportunity."
("Unique" sounds like "yoo-neek" → consonant sound → use "a")

**Situation 3:** Acronyms
Score 400-500: "We need a HR representative at the meeting."
Score 650+: "We need an HR representative at the meeting."
("HR" = "aitch-ar" → starts with vowel sound → use "an")

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Choosing articles based on spelling only ✗
• Using "an" before "university," "European," "one" ✗
• Using "a" before "hour," "honor," "MBA," "MRI" ✗

**Use:**
• Say the word out loud—listen to the first SOUND ✓
• Vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u sound) → an ✓
• Consonant sound → a ✓
• Common tricky words: an hour, a university, an MBA, a European ✓
