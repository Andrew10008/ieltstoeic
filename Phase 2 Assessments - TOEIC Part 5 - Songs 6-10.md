## Assessment 26
**Assessment number:** 26
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Noun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb Forms (Same Root)

**Brief meaning:**
• **manage** (verb: to manage the project)
• **manager** (noun: the manager)
• **management** (noun: the management team)
• **managerial** (adjective: managerial skills)

**Pre-listening:** "Many TOEIC Part 5 questions test whether you can identify the correct word form (noun, verb, adjective, or adverb) based on sentence structure. Words like 'manage/manager/management/managerial' all come from the same root but serve different grammatical functions."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 choose words based on meaning alone, ignoring grammatical function. Those scoring 650+ identify what type of word the sentence needs (noun, verb, adjective, or adverb) before choosing.

**Score 400-500:** "She has excellent manager skills." ✗
**Score 650+:** "She has excellent managerial skills." ✓

After "excellent" (adjective), before "skills" (noun), you need another adjective → "managerial."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Identifying the verb position
Score 400-500: "The team will management the project."
Score 650+: "The team will manage the project."
(After "will" → need base verb "manage")

**Situation 2:** Identifying the noun position
Score 400-500: "Effective manage requires clear communication."
Score 650+: "Effective management requires clear communication."
(Subject position → need noun "management")

**Situation 3:** Identifying the adjective position
Score 400-500: "He demonstrated strong management abilities."
Score 650+: "He demonstrated strong managerial abilities."
(Before noun "abilities" → need adjective "managerial")

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Choosing words based only on meaning ✗
• Ignoring grammatical signals (position, surrounding words) ✗
• Not knowing word form patterns (noun, verb, adjective, adverb) ✗

**Use:**
• Identify what the sentence needs: noun, verb, adjective, or adverb ✓
• Verb position: after will/can/should, or as main verb ✓
• Noun position: subject, object, after articles (a/an/the) ✓
• Adjective position: before nouns, after linking verbs ✓
• Adverb position: modifying verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "The manager team meets weekly." → "The management team meets weekly."

## Assessment 27
**Assessment number:** 27
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Verb vs. Noun Forms in Context

**Brief meaning:**
• **decide** (verb: we will decide)
• **decision** (noun: make a decision)
• **perform** (verb: they perform)
• **performance** (noun: excellent performance)

**Pre-listening:** "Distinguishing between verb and noun forms is critical in TOEIC Part 5. Verbs follow subjects and auxiliaries (will, can, should), while nouns follow articles (a, an, the) and come before/after verbs as subjects or objects."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers confuse verb and noun forms. Higher-scoring test-takers recognize structural clues: articles signal nouns, while auxiliaries signal verbs.

**Score 400-500:** "We need to make a decide soon." ✗
**Score 650+:** "We need to make a decision soon." ✓

After "a" (article) → need noun "decision."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** After auxiliary verbs
Score 400-500: "The team will decision tomorrow."
Score 650+: "The team will decide tomorrow."
(After "will" → need verb "decide")

**Situation 2:** As subject
Score 400-500: "The perform of the employees was excellent."
Score 650+: "The performance of the employees was excellent."
(Subject with "the" → need noun "performance")

**Situation 3:** After "to" (infinitive)
Score 400-500: "It's important to performance well."
Score 650+: "It's important to perform well."
(After "to" in infinitive → need verb "perform")

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using nouns after auxiliaries (will, can, should, must) ✗
• Using verbs after articles (a, an, the) ✗
• Confusing "make a decide" instead of "make a decision" ✗

**Use:**
• Articles (a, an, the) + NOUN ✓
• Auxiliaries (will, can, should) + VERB ✓
• Subject position → usually NOUN ✓
• After "to" (infinitive) → VERB ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "The company will make a decide about the merger." → "The company will make a decision about the merger."

**Recording Tips:**

## Assessment 30
**Assessment number:** 30
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Context Clues for Word Forms

**Brief meaning:**
• **after articles** → noun
• **after modals** → verb
• **before nouns** → adjective
• **after verbs** → adverb

**Pre-listening:** "TOEIC Part 5 word form questions can be solved by recognizing structural patterns. Articles (a, an, the) signal nouns, modals (will, can, should) signal verbs, positions before nouns signal adjectives, and positions after action verbs often signal adverbs."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 read the whole sentence multiple times without a strategy. Those scoring 650+ use quick pattern recognition to identify what word form is needed.

**Score 400-500:** Reading the sentence slowly and guessing based on meaning.
**Score 650+:** Spotting "the ___ of" pattern → automatically know a NOUN is needed.

Looking at structure first saves time and improves accuracy.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** "The ___ of..." pattern
Score 400-500: "The improve of our services is ongoing."
Score 650+: "The improvement of our services is ongoing."
("The ___ of" → noun pattern)

**Situation 2:** "Will ___ the" pattern
Score 400-500: "The manager will discussion the proposal."
Score 650+: "The manager will discuss the proposal."
("Will ___ the" → verb pattern)

**Situation 3:** "A ___ solution" pattern
Score 400-500: "We found a create solution."
Score 650+: "We found a creative solution."
("A ___ noun" → adjective pattern)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Reading the entire sentence before identifying word type needed ✗
• Ignoring structural clues (articles, modals, position) ✗
• Choosing based on meaning alone ✗

**Use:**
• Quick pattern recognition: the ___ of = NOUN ✓
• Will/can/should ___ = VERB ✓
• A/an/the ___ NOUN = ADJECTIVE ✓
• VERB ___ (no object) = ADVERB ✓
• Save time by spotting patterns first ✓

**Recording Task:**
Identify and transform: "The company's financially ___ (stable) has improved." → "The company's financial stability has improved."

## Assessment 31
**Assessment number:** 31
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Affect (Verb) vs. Effect (Noun)

**Brief meaning:**
• **affect** (verb: to influence) - "The changes will affect productivity."
• **effect** (noun: result) - "The effect of the changes was positive."
• **have an effect on** (noun phrase)
• **be affected by** (passive verb)

**Pre-listening:** "'Affect' is almost always a VERB meaning 'to influence,' while 'effect' is almost always a NOUN meaning 'result' or 'outcome.' This is one of the most frequently tested word pairs in TOEIC Part 5."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 confuse these words because they sound similar. Those scoring 650+ use a simple rule: affect = verb (action), effect = noun (result).

**Score 400-500:** "The new policy will have a significant affect on employees." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The new policy will have a significant effect on employees." ✓

After "a significant" (article + adjective) → need noun "effect."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Verb position
Score 400-500: "Budget cuts may effect our hiring plans."
Score 650+: "Budget cuts may affect our hiring plans."
(After "may" → need verb "affect")

**Situation 2:** Noun position with article
Score 400-500: "The affect of the training was immediate."
Score 650+: "The effect of the training was immediate."
(After "the" → need noun "effect")

**Situation 3:** Passive voice
Score 400-500: "Sales were effected by the economic downturn."
Score 650+: "Sales were affected by the economic downturn."
(Passive verb form → "affected")

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "effect" as a verb (99% of the time) ✗
• Using "affect" as a noun ✗
• Choosing based on sound alone ✗

**Use:**
• AFFECT = verb (to affect, will affect, was affected) ✓
• EFFECT = noun (the effect, an effect, effects) ✓
• Phrase: "have an effect ON" ✓
• Phrase: "be affected BY" ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "How will the changes affect/effect your department? The affect/effect should be minimal." → "How will the changes affect your department? The effect should be minimal."

## Assessment 32
**Assessment number:** 32
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Principal (Main) vs. Principle (Rule/Belief)

**Brief meaning:**
• **principal** (adjective/noun: main, or a leader) - "the principal reason," "the school principal"
• **principle** (noun: fundamental rule) - "basic principles," "as a matter of principle"
• **in principle** (phrase: theoretically)

**Pre-listening:** "'Principal' means 'main' or 'most important' (adjective) or refers to a leader (noun). 'Principle' means a fundamental truth, rule, or belief (noun only). Remember: principal = pal (person), principle = rule."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers guess randomly between these homophones. Higher-scoring test-takers use meaning: principal = main/leader, principle = rule/belief.

**Score 400-500:** "The principle objective is to increase sales." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The principal objective is to increase sales." ✓

Meaning "main objective" → use "principal."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Meaning "main" or "most important"
Score 400-500: "Our principle concern is customer satisfaction."
Score 650+: "Our principal concern is customer satisfaction."
(Meaning "main concern" → "principal")

**Situation 2:** Meaning "fundamental rule or belief"
Score 400-500: "The company operates on ethical principals."
Score 650+: "The company operates on ethical principles."
(Meaning "rules/beliefs" → "principles")

**Situation 3:** Fixed phrase "in principle"
Score 400-500: "We agree in principal."
Score 650+: "We agree in principle."
(Fixed phrase → "principle")

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Confusing the two spellings ✗
• Using "principle" to mean "main" ✗
• Using "principal" to mean "rule" ✗

**Use:**
• PRINCIPAL = main, chief, most important (adjective) ✓
• PRINCIPAL = leader (noun: school principal, company principal) ✓
• PRINCIPLE = fundamental rule, truth, or belief (noun) ✓
• Memory trick: principal = pal (person/main); principle = rule ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "The principle/principal reason for success is following our core principle/principals." → "The principal reason for success is following our core principles."

**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 confuse noun and verb forms because they sound similar. Those scoring 650+ use grammatical position: after articles/adjectives = advice (noun), after subject = advise (verb).

**Score 400-500:** "Thank you for your advise." ✗

(After "valuable" → noun "advice")

**Situation 2:** Verb position
Score 400-500: "I would advice you to reconsider."
Score 650+: "I would advise you to reconsider."
(After "would" → verb "advise")

**Situation 3:** "Please advise" (common business phrase)
Score 400-500: "Please advice us of any changes."
Score 650+: "Please advise us of any changes."
(Verb form in command → "advise")

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "advise" as a noun ✗
• Using "advice" as a verb ✗
• Saying "an advice" (advice is uncountable) ✗

**Use:**
• ADVICE = noun (some advice, good advice, a piece of advice) ✓
• ADVISE = verb (to advise, please advise, I advise) ✓

(Meaning "excluding" → preposition "except")

**Situation 3:** "Accept responsibility"
Score 400-500: "The company will except full responsibility."
Score 650+: "The company will accept full responsibility."
(Meaning "take on" → verb "accept")

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "except" as a verb ✗
• Using "accept" as a preposition ✗
• Confusing based on spelling similarity ✗

**Use:**
• ACCEPT = verb (to accept, will accept, accepted) ✓

## Assessment 36
**Assessment number:** 36
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Make vs. Do (Common Collocations)

**Brief meaning:**
• **make** a decision, a choice, a mistake, progress, money, an effort
• **do** homework, business, research, a favor, the job, work
• **make an appointment** (not "do")
• **do your best** (not "make")

**Pre-listening:** "'Make' and 'do' are not interchangeable in English. 'Make' generally relates to creating, producing, or causing. 'Do' generally relates to performing, completing, or acting. TOEIC tests fixed collocations that must be memorized."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 translate from their native language or guess randomly. Those scoring 650+ have memorized common make/do collocations and recognize them automatically.

**Score 400-500:** "We need to do a decision by Friday." ✗
**Score 650+:** "We need to make a decision by Friday." ✓

"Make a decision" is the correct fixed collocation.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Business actions
Score 400-500: "The company is making business internationally."
Score 650+: "The company is doing business internationally."
("Do business" is the fixed phrase)

**Situation 2:** Creating/producing
Score 400-500: "She did an excellent presentation."
Score 650+: "She made an excellent presentation."
("Make a presentation" = create/produce)

**Situation 3:** Performing tasks
Score 400-500: "Please make your homework before class."
Score 650+: "Please do your homework before class."
("Do homework" = perform a task)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Translating make/do directly from your language ✗
• Thinking make and do are interchangeable ✗
• Guessing without knowing the fixed phrase ✗

**Use:**
• MAKE: decision, choice, mistake, progress, money, effort, appointment ✓
• DO: homework, business, research, favor, job, work, best ✓
• Memorize phrases as chunks, not individual words ✓
• Keep a collocation notebook ✓

**Recording Task:**

## Assessment 41
**Assessment number:** 41
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Contrast Connectors (However, Nevertheless, Although, Despite)

**Brief meaning:**
• **however** (sentence connector, formal) - "Sales fell. However, profits increased."
• **nevertheless** (sentence connector, emphasis) - "It's expensive. Nevertheless, it's worth it."
• **although** (subordinating conjunction) - "Although sales fell, profits increased."
• **despite** (preposition + noun) - "Despite the challenges, we succeeded."

**Pre-listening:** "Contrast connectors show opposite ideas. TOEIC tests whether you know the grammatical differences: 'however' starts a new sentence, 'although' connects clauses, and 'despite' needs a noun or -ing form."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 confuse these because they all mean "but." Those scoring 650+ choose based on grammar: sentence structure determines which connector to use.

**Score 400-500:** "Although the cost, we proceeded." ✗
**Score 650+:** "Despite the cost, we proceeded." ✓

"The cost" is a noun → use "despite" (preposition), not "although" (needs a clause).

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Connecting two independent sentences
Score 400-500: "The market is uncertain. Although, we will invest."
Score 650+: "The market is uncertain. However, we will invest."
(Two complete sentences → use "however" or "nevertheless")

**Situation 2:** With a complete clause (subject + verb)
Score 400-500: "Despite it was late, we continued working."
Score 650+: "Although it was late, we continued working."
(Complete clause "it was late" → use "although," not "despite")

**Situation 3:** With a noun or -ing form
Score 400-500: "However the rain, the event continued."
Score 650+: "Despite the rain, the event continued."
(Noun "rain" → use "despite")

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "although" with just a noun ✗
• Using "despite" with a subject + verb clause ✗
• Starting with "Although" as a sentence connector ✗

**Use:**
• HOWEVER/NEVERTHELESS: sentence + period + however/nevertheless + comma ✓
• ALTHOUGH: although + subject + verb, main clause ✓
• DESPITE: despite + noun/gerund (-ing), main clause ✓
• Despite = in spite of (same grammar) ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "Although the difficult conditions, the team succeeded. However it was challenging." → "Despite the difficult conditions, the team succeeded. Although it was challenging."

## Assessment 42
**Assessment number:** 42
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Cause and Effect Connectors (Therefore, Consequently, Because, Since)

**Brief meaning:**
• **therefore** (sentence connector) - "Sales fell. Therefore, we cut costs."
• **consequently** (sentence connector) - "Demand increased. Consequently, we hired more staff."
• **because** (subordinating conjunction) - "We expanded because demand increased."
• **since** (subordinating conjunction) - "Since costs rose, we raised prices."

**Pre-listening:** "Cause-effect connectors show reasons and results. TOEIC tests whether you know 'therefore/consequently' connect independent sentences, while 'because/since' connect clauses within one sentence."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers use these interchangeably. Higher-scoring test-takers know: therefore/consequently = sentence connectors (after period/semicolon); because/since = clause connectors.

**Score 400-500:** "The project was urgent, therefore we worked overtime." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The project was urgent. Therefore, we worked overtime." OR "The project was urgent; therefore, we worked overtime." ✓

"Therefore" needs a period or semicolon before it, not just a comma.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Showing result (two sentences)
Score 400-500: "Costs increased, consequently prices rose."
Score 650+: "Costs increased. Consequently, prices rose."
(Two independent ideas → use period before "consequently")

**Situation 2:** Showing reason (one sentence)
Score 400-500: "Therefore demand decreased, we reduced production."
Score 650+: "Because demand decreased, we reduced production."
(Connecting clauses in one sentence → use "because" or "since")

**Situation 3:** Formal result
Score 400-500: "Because sales grew, therefore we expanded."
Score 650+: "Because sales grew, we expanded." OR "Sales grew. Therefore, we expanded."
(Don't use both "because" and "therefore" together)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "therefore" with just a comma (needs period/semicolon) ✗
• Using "because" at the start of a new sentence ✗
• Combining "because" and "therefore" in one sentence ✗

**Use:**
• THEREFORE/CONSEQUENTLY: sentence. Therefore, result. ✓
• BECAUSE/SINCE: because/since reason, result OR result because/since reason ✓
• Therefore/consequently = more formal than "so" ✓
• Because/since = more formal than "cause" ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "Sales dropped, therefore we reduced inventory because costs rose." → "Sales dropped. Therefore, we reduced inventory because costs rose."
