## Song 11: "Who, Which, That, and Whose" (Relative Pronouns)

## Assessment 51
**Assessment number:** 51
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Who vs. Which vs. That (Subject and Object Pronouns)

**Brief meaning:**
• **who** (for people, subject or object) - "the employee who manages," "the person who we hired"
• **which** (for things, subject or object) - "the report which explains," "the system which we use"
• **that** (for people or things, subject or object) - "the manager that approved," "the solution that works"

**Pre-listening:** "Relative pronouns connect clauses and refer back to nouns. 'Who' is for people, 'which' is for things, and 'that' can be for either. TOEIC tests whether you choose the correct pronoun based on whether you're referring to a person or a thing."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 confuse who/which/that. Those scoring 650+ automatically use 'who' for people and 'which' for things, with 'that' as a versatile alternative for both.

**Score 400-500:** "The system who processes data is efficient." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The system that processes data is efficient." OR "The system which processes data is efficient." ✓

"System" is a thing → use "which" or "that," not "who."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Referring to people
Score 400-500: "The manager which approved the budget has resigned."
Score 650+: "The manager who approved the budget has resigned."
(Person → use "who")

**Situation 2:** Referring to things
Score 400-500: "The document who contains the data is confidential."
Score 650+: "The document that contains the data is confidential."
(Thing → use "that" or "which")

**Situation 3:** Versatile "that"
Score 400-500: Unsure whether to use "that"
Score 650+: "The employee that submitted the report" OR "The report that was submitted"
("That" works for both people and things)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "who" for things ✗
• Using "which" for people ✗
• Being afraid to use "that" (it's versatile!) ✗

**Use:**
• WHO: for people (the person who, employees who, anyone who) ✓
• WHICH: for things (the report which, systems which, data which) ✓
• THAT: for people OR things (versatile, slightly less formal) ✓
• In formal writing, prefer who/which; in everyday use, "that" is fine ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "The employee which submitted the proposal and the report who was approved are ready." → "The employee who submitted the proposal and the report that was approved are ready."

## Assessment 52
**Assessment number:** 52
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Whose (Possessive Relative Pronoun)

**Brief meaning:**
• **whose** (possessive for people and things) - "the manager whose team won," "the company whose profits increased"
• **NOT "who's"** (who is/who has)

**Pre-listening:** "'Whose' shows possession or relationship in relative clauses. It can refer to people ('the person whose idea') or things ('the company whose revenue'). Don't confuse it with 'who's' (who is). TOEIC tests this distinction."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers confuse "whose" (possessive) with "who's" (who is). Higher-scoring test-takers recognize that "whose" shows ownership or association.

**Score 400-500:** "The employee who's proposal was accepted will present." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The employee whose proposal was accepted will present." ✓

Showing possession of the proposal → use "whose," not "who's."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Possession for people
Score 400-500: "The manager which office is on the third floor is unavailable."
Score 650+: "The manager whose office is on the third floor is unavailable."
(Possession → "whose")

**Situation 2:** Association for companies/things
Score 400-500: "The company that's headquarters are in Tokyo is expanding."
Score 650+: "The company whose headquarters are in Tokyo is expanding."
(Association/possession → "whose")

**Situation 3:** Avoiding "who's" confusion
Score 400-500: "The person who's been hired" (correct: who has)
Score 650+: "The person who's been hired" ✓ OR "The person whose experience impressed us" ✓
(who's = who has/who is; whose = possession)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "who's" (who is) when you mean "whose" (possession) ✗
• Using "that" or "which" for possession ✗
• Thinking "whose" is only for people ✗

**Use:**
• WHOSE = possession/association (for people AND things) ✓
• Pattern: noun + whose + noun + verb (the manager whose team wins) ✓
• Test: Can you replace with "his/her/its"? Then use "whose" ✓
• "The manager whose team" = "the manager...his/her team" ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "The company who's profits increased and the employee who's idea succeeded are celebrating." → "The company whose profits increased and the employee whose idea succeeded are celebrating."

## Assessment 53
**Assessment number:** 53
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses (Comma Usage)

**Brief meaning:**
• **defining clause** (essential, no commas) - "Employees who work overtime receive extra pay." (only those who work overtime)
• **non-defining clause** (extra info, commas) - "The CEO, who started last month, announced changes." (the CEO happens to have started last month)

**Pre-listening:** "Defining clauses identify which person/thing you mean (essential information, no commas). Non-defining clauses add extra information about someone/something already identified (use commas). TOEIC tests comma placement with relative clauses."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 don't understand when to use commas. Those scoring 650+ know: if the clause is essential to identify the noun, no commas; if it's just extra info, use commas.

**Score 400-500:** "The manager, who approved the budget, is here." (when you mean the specific manager who approved it)
**Score 650+:** "The manager who approved the budget is here." ✓ (identifying which manager)

No commas because the clause identifies WHICH manager.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Defining (identifying which one)
Score 400-500: "The employees, who completed training, can use the equipment."
Score 650+: "The employees who completed training can use the equipment."
(Only those who completed training → defining, no commas)

**Situation 2:** Non-defining (extra information)
Score 400-500: "Our CEO who joined in January announced the merger."
Score 650+: "Our CEO, who joined in January, announced the merger."
(We have one CEO, "joined in January" is extra info → commas)

**Situation 3:** Meaning changes with commas
"Employees who arrive late will be warned." = Only late arrivers will be warned
"Employees, who arrive late, will be warned." = All employees (who happen to arrive late) will be warned

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using commas when the clause is essential ✗
• Omitting commas when adding extra information ✗
• Not understanding that commas change meaning ✗

**Use:**
• Defining (which one?) → NO commas ✓
• Non-defining (extra info about already-identified noun) → USE commas ✓
• If you can remove the clause and sentence loses essential meaning → NO commas ✓
• If clause is removable without losing main meaning → USE commas ✓

**Recording Task:**
Add/remove commas correctly: "The report which I submitted yesterday which contains the sales data has been approved." → "The report which I submitted yesterday, which contains the sales data, has been approved." (First clause defines which report; second adds extra info)

## Assessment 54
**Assessment number:** 54
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** When to Omit Relative Pronouns

**Brief meaning:**
• **Can omit** (when pronoun is object) - "The report (that) I wrote..." ✓
• **Cannot omit** (when pronoun is subject) - "The report that explains..." ✗ can't omit
• **Never omit "whose"**

**Pre-listening:** "In conversational English, we often omit relative pronouns when they're the object of the clause ('the person I met' = 'the person whom I met'). But we CANNOT omit the pronoun when it's the subject ('the person who called' cannot be 'the person called'). TOEIC tests this distinction."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers don't know when pronouns can be omitted. Higher-scoring test-takers ask: Is the pronoun the subject or object? Object → can omit. Subject → cannot omit.

**Score 400-500:** "The manager sent the email is Mr. Kim." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The manager who sent the email is Mr. Kim." ✓

"Who" is the subject of "sent" → cannot omit.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Object pronoun (can omit)
Score 400-500: Not knowing they can omit
Score 650+: "The report (that) we submitted was approved." ✓
("That" is object of "submitted" → can omit)

**Situation 2:** Subject pronoun (cannot omit)
Score 400-500: "The employee works here is experienced."
Score 650+: "The employee who works here is experienced."
("Who" is subject of "works" → cannot omit)

**Situation 3:** Test for omission
Ask: What's the subject of the verb in the relative clause?
"The person [that] I hired" → subject of "hired" = I → "that" is object → can omit
"The person [that] arrived" → subject of "arrived" = that → cannot omit

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Omitting subject pronouns ✗
• Thinking all relative pronouns must stay ✗
• Omitting "whose" (never omit) ✗

**Use:**
• Object pronoun → can omit (the person [that] I met) ✓
• Subject pronoun → cannot omit (the person who arrived) ✓
• Test: If pronoun is not doing the action → likely object → can omit ✓
• WHOSE: never omit ✓

**Recording Task:**
Identify if pronoun can be omitted: "The manager (who) I met yesterday (who) oversees our department is very approachable." → "The manager I met yesterday who oversees our department is very approachable." (First "who" can be omitted; second cannot)

## Assessment 55
**Assessment number:** 55
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Prepositions with Relative Pronouns (Formal vs. Informal)

**Brief meaning:**
• **informal** - "The company (that) I work for..."
• **formal** - "The company for which I work..."
• **never** - "The company for that I work..." ✗

**Pre-listening:** "In formal writing, prepositions can come before relative pronouns ('the issue about which we spoke'). In everyday English, prepositions come at the end ('the issue that we spoke about'). TOEIC tests whether you recognize that 'that' cannot follow a preposition."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 may not recognize formal structures. Those scoring 650+ know that formal structures use "which/whom" after prepositions, never "that."

**Score 400-500:** "The project for that we are responsible is behind schedule." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The project for which we are responsible is behind schedule." OR "The project that we are responsible for is behind schedule." ✓

After a preposition, use "which" or "whom," never "that."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Informal structure (preposition at end)
Score 400-500: "The client with who we work is satisfied."
Score 650+: "The client (that) we work with is satisfied."
(Informal → preposition at end, can omit "that")

**Situation 2:** Formal structure (preposition before pronoun)
Score 400-500: "The issue about that we're concerned is being addressed."
Score 650+: "The issue about which we're concerned is being addressed."
(Formal → preposition before "which," not "that")

**Situation 3:** With "whom" (for people)
Score 400-500: "The colleague with who I collaborated has left."
Score 650+: "The colleague with whom I collaborated has left."
(After preposition, people → "whom," not "who")

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "that" after a preposition ✗
• Using "who" after a preposition (use "whom") ✗
• Mixing formal and informal styles ✗

**Use:**
• Informal: preposition at end (the person [that] I work with) ✓
• Formal: preposition before pronoun (the person with whom I work) ✓
• After preposition: which (things) or whom (people), never "that" ✓
• TOEIC prefers recognizing both structures ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform to formal: "The principle that we operate by is integrity." → "The principle by which we operate is integrity."

## Assessment 56
**Assessment number:** 56
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Verbs Followed by Infinitives (To + Verb)

**Brief meaning:**
• **want to**, **need to**, **plan to**, **hope to**, **expect to**
• **decide to**, **agree to**, **refuse to**, **promise to**
• **would like to**, **seem to**, **appear to**

**Pre-listening:** "Certain verbs are always followed by infinitives (to + base verb). These include verbs about desires (want, hope), decisions (decide, plan), and agreements (agree, promise). TOEIC tests whether you recognize which verbs require infinitives."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 guess randomly between infinitives and gerunds. Those scoring 650+ have memorized which verbs take infinitives and recognize them automatically.

**Score 400-500:** "We plan expanding our operations next year." ✗
**Score 650+:** "We plan to expand our operations next year." ✓

"Plan" is always followed by infinitive → "to expand."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Expressing desires/wants
Score 400-500: "The company hopes increasing its market share."
Score 650+: "The company hopes to increase its market share."
("Hope" + infinitive)

**Situation 2:** Making decisions
Score 400-500: "Management decided implementing the changes."
Score 650+: "Management decided to implement the changes."
("Decide" + infinitive)

**Situation 3:** Making promises
Score 400-500: "We promise delivering high-quality service."
Score 650+: "We promise to deliver high-quality service."
("Promise" + infinitive)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using gerunds after want, need, plan, hope, expect ✗
• Using gerunds after decide, agree, refuse, promise ✗
• Guessing instead of memorizing patterns ✗

**Use:**
• INFINITIVE verbs: want, need, plan, hope, expect, decide, agree, refuse, promise ✓
• Pattern: verb + TO + base verb ✓
• Memory group: "Decision/Future verbs" → infinitive ✓
• Would like to, seem to, appear to → always infinitive ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "We need expanding our team and hope hiring three new employees." → "We need to expand our team and hope to hire three new employees."

**Brief meaning:**
• **enjoy**, **finish**, **avoid**, **consider**, **suggest**
• **recommend**, **deny**, **admit**, **practice**, **risk**
• **mind**, **miss**, **postpone**, **delay**

**Pre-listening:** "Certain verbs are always followed by gerunds (verb + -ing). These include verbs about activities (enjoy, finish, practice), mental processes (consider, imagine), and avoidance (avoid, risk). TOEIC frequently tests gerund patterns."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers confuse infinitives and gerunds. Higher-scoring test-takers know that gerund verbs relate to actual, ongoing, or past activities rather than future intentions.

**Score 400-500:** "We enjoyed to work on this project." ✗
**Score 650+:** "We enjoyed working on this project." ✓

"Enjoy" is always followed by gerund → "working."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Expressing enjoyment/completion

("Finish" + gerund)

**Situation 2:** Giving suggestions/recommendations
Score 400-500: "I suggest to review the contract carefully."
Score 650+: "I suggest reviewing the contract carefully."
("Suggest" + gerund)

**Situation 3:** Considering options
Score 400-500: "We're considering to hire a consultant."
Score 650+: "We're considering hiring a consultant."
("Consider" + gerund)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using infinitives after enjoy, finish, avoid, consider, suggest ✗
• Using infinitives after mind, miss, postpone, deny, admit ✗
• Not memorizing gerund verb lists ✗

**Use:**
• GERUND verbs: enjoy, finish, avoid, consider, suggest, recommend, mind ✓
• Pattern: verb + VERB-ing ✓
• Memory group: "Activity/Experience verbs" → gerund ✓
• Common business: avoid making, consider hiring, recommend implementing ✓

**Recording Task:**

## Assessment 58
**Assessment number:** 58
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Verbs That Take Both Infinitives and Gerunds

**Brief meaning:**
• **start to do / start doing** (same meaning)
• **begin to do / begin doing** (same meaning)
• **continue to do / continue doing** (same meaning)
• **like to do / like doing** (slight difference)
• **love to do / love doing** (same meaning)

**Pre-listening:** "Some verbs can be followed by either infinitives or gerunds with little or no change in meaning. These include start, begin, continue, like, love, and hate. TOEIC may test whether you recognize both forms are correct."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 think only one form is correct. Those scoring 650+ know that both forms are acceptable with these verbs, though infinitives may sound slightly more formal.

**Score 400-500:** Thinking "We started working" is wrong because they learned "start to work."
**Score 650+:** "We started to work" ✓ AND "We started working" ✓ (both correct)

Both forms are grammatically correct and mean the same thing.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Beginning an action
Score 400-500: Thinking only one is correct
Score 650+: "The company began to expand" = "The company began expanding" ✓
(Both correct)

**Situation 2:** Continuing an action
Score 400-500: Thinking only one is correct
Score 650+: "We'll continue to monitor the situation" = "We'll continue monitoring the situation" ✓
(Both correct)

**Situation 3:** Expressing preferences
Score 400-500: Confusing the two
Score 650+: "I like to work independently" (general preference) ≈ "I like working independently" (enjoy the experience)
(Very slight difference, both acceptable)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Thinking both forms can't be correct ✗
• Spending too much time choosing between them ✗
• Not knowing this pattern exists ✗

**Use:**
• START/BEGIN/CONTINUE: infinitive or gerund (same meaning) ✓
• LIKE/LOVE/HATE: infinitive or gerund (virtually same meaning) ✓
• PREFER: usually "prefer to" or "prefer verb-ing" ✓
• If both sound natural, both are likely correct ✓

**Recording Task:**
Accept both forms: "The team started implementing / started to implement the new system." (Both correct!)

## Assessment 59
**Assessment number:** 59
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Preposition + Gerund (Never Infinitive)

**Brief meaning:**
• **interested in doing** (not "to do")
• **good at doing** (not "to do")
• **responsible for doing** (not "to do")
• **look forward to doing** (not "to do")

**Pre-listening:** "After prepositions (in, at, for, to, about, of), always use gerunds, never infinitives. This includes phrasal expressions like 'look forward to,' 'be used to,' and 'in addition to.' The 'to' in these phrases is a preposition, not part of an infinitive."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers use infinitives after "to" regardless of whether it's a preposition. Higher-scoring test-takers recognize when "to" is a preposition (followed by gerund) vs. part of infinitive.

**Score 400-500:** "We look forward to meet you." ✗
**Score 650+:** "We look forward to meeting you." ✓

"To" in "look forward to" is a preposition → followed by gerund.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** "Be interested in"
Score 400-500: "We're interested in to expand our services."
Score 650+: "We're interested in expanding our services."
("In" is a preposition → gerund)

**Situation 2:** "Be responsible for"
Score 400-500: "She's responsible for to manage the team."
Score 650+: "She's responsible for managing the team."
("For" is a preposition → gerund)

**Situation 3:** "Look forward to" (tricky!)
Score 400-500: "We look forward to receive your response."
Score 650+: "We look forward to receiving your response."
("To" is a preposition here, not infinitive marker → gerund)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using infinitives after prepositions ✗
• Thinking "to" always starts an infinitive ✗
• Not recognizing "look forward to + gerund" pattern ✗

**Use:**
• After prepositions → ALWAYS gerund ✓
• Interested IN doing, good AT doing, responsible FOR doing ✓
• Look forward TO doing, be used TO doing, in addition TO doing ✓
• Test: Is "to" part of "look forward to"? → gerund. Is "to" before a base verb? → infinitive ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "We're interested in to participate and look forward to hear from you." → "We're interested in participating and look forward to hearing from you."

## Assessment 60
**Assessment number:** 60
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Verbs with Meaning Changes (Remember, Stop, Try, Forget)

**Brief meaning:**
• **remember to do** (remember, then do) vs. **remember doing** (recall past action)
• **stop to do** (stop in order to do) vs. **stop doing** (cease doing)
• **try to do** (attempt) vs. **try doing** (experiment)
• **forget to do** (forget, so didn't do) vs. **forget doing** (don't recall doing)

**Pre-listening:** "A few verbs change meaning depending on whether they're followed by infinitives or gerunds. These include remember, forget, stop, and try. TOEIC tests whether you understand the meaning difference."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 use these interchangeably. Those scoring 650+ understand the crucial meaning differences and choose based on intended meaning.

**Score 400-500:** "I stopped checking my email" vs. "I stopped to check my email" (not understanding the difference)
**Score 650+:** "I stopped checking" = I ceased checking ✓ / "I stopped to check" = I paused in order to check ✓

The grammar choice completely changes the meaning.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** "Remember"
Score 400-500: Confusing the meanings
Score 650+:
- "Remember to send the report" = Don't forget to send it (future action)
- "I remember sending the report" = I recall that I sent it (past action)

**Situation 2:** "Stop"
Score 400-500: Not seeing the difference
Score 650+:
- "We stopped to have lunch" = We paused in order to have lunch
- "We stopped having lunch meetings" = We ceased having lunch meetings

**Situation 3:** "Try"
Score 400-500: Using randomly
Score 650+:
- "Try to finish on time" = Attempt to finish, make an effort
- "Try finishing early" = Experiment with finishing early, see if it works

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using infinitive/gerund interchangeably with these verbs ✗
• Not knowing that meaning changes ✗
• Guessing instead of learning the patterns ✗

**Use:**
• REMEMBER TO do = don't forget (future) ✓
• REMEMBER doing = recall (past) ✓
• STOP TO do = pause in order to do ✓
• STOP doing = cease doing ✓
• TRY TO do = attempt ✓
• TRY doing = experiment ✓

**Recording Task:**
Choose correctly: "Don't forget ___  (submit) the report" vs. "I'll never forget ___ (work) with this team." → "Don't forget to submit the report" (future action) vs. "I'll never forget working with this team" (past experience)

## Assessment 61
**Assessment number:** 61
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Basic Passive Structure (Be + Past Participle)

**Brief meaning:**
• **is done** (present simple passive)
• **was done** (past simple passive)
• **will be done** (future passive)
• **has been done** (present perfect passive)

**Pre-listening:** "Passive voice shifts focus from who does the action to what receives the action. Structure: be (in any tense) + past participle. TOEIC tests whether you can recognize and form correct passive structures."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 confuse active and passive or use incorrect passive forms. Those scoring 650+ automatically recognize when passive is appropriate and form it correctly.

**Score 400-500:** "The report completed yesterday." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The report was completed yesterday." ✓

Passive structure requires "be" verb → "was completed."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Present simple passive
Score 400-500: "The meeting holds every Monday."
Score 650+: "The meeting is held every Monday."
(Passive: is + held)

**Situation 2:** Past simple passive
Score 400-500: "The decision made last week."
Score 650+: "The decision was made last week."
(Passive: was + made)

**Situation 3:** Future passive
Score 400-500: "The results announce tomorrow."
Score 650+: "The results will be announced tomorrow."
(Passive: will be + announced)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Omitting "be" verb in passive ✗
• Using wrong form of past participle ✗
• Forgetting to match "be" to the tense ✗

**Use:**
• Structure: BE (correct tense) + PAST PARTICIPLE ✓
• Present: is/are + past participle ✓
• Past: was/were + past participle ✓
• Future: will be + past participle ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform to passive: "The company announces the results. The team completed the project." → "The results are announced by the company. The project was completed by the team."

## Assessment 62
**Assessment number:** 62
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Passive with Modal Verbs (Can, Should, Must, May)

**Brief meaning:**
• **can be done**
• **should be done**
• **must be done**
• **may be done**

**Pre-listening:** "Passive voice with modals follows the pattern: modal + be + past participle. Common in business English for policies, procedures, and requirements. TOEIC frequently tests modal passive structures."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers forget the "be" or use wrong forms. Higher-scoring test-takers know the pattern: modal + be + past participle (never "modal + been").

**Score 400-500:** "The form should submitted by Friday." ✗ OR "The form should been submitted by Friday." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The form should be submitted by Friday." ✓

Modal passive: should + be + submitted.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Ability (can)
Score 400-500: "The task can completed in one day."
Score 650+: "The task can be completed in one day."
(can + be + completed)

**Situation 2:** Recommendation (should)
Score 400-500: "Applications should sent to the HR department."
Score 650+: "Applications should be sent to the HR department."
(should + be + sent)

**Situation 3:** Requirement (must)
Score 400-500: "All documents must signed by the manager."
Score 650+: "All documents must be signed by the manager."
(must + be + signed)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "modal + past participle" without "be" ✗
• Using "modal + been + past participle" ✗
• Confusing with present perfect passive ✗

**Use:**
• Pattern: MODAL + BE + PAST PARTICIPLE ✓
• Can be done, should be done, must be done, may be done ✓
• Common in procedures: "must be completed," "should be reviewed" ✓

## Assessment 63
**Assessment number:** 63
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Continuous and Perfect Passive Forms

**Brief meaning:**
• **is being done** (present continuous passive)
• **was being done** (past continuous passive)
• **has been done** (present perfect passive)
• **had been done** (past perfect passive)

**Pre-listening:** "Continuous passive (is being done) emphasizes ongoing process. Perfect passive (has been done) emphasizes completion or current state. TOEIC tests whether you can form these more complex passive structures."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 avoid complex passive forms or make errors. Those scoring 650+ recognize patterns: continuous = be + being + past participle; perfect = have/has/had + been + past participle.

**Score 400-500:** "The building is building right now." ✗
**Score 650+:** "The building is being built right now." ✓

Present continuous passive: is + being + built.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Present continuous passive (ongoing now)
Score 400-500: "The issue is addressing by management."
Score 650+: "The issue is being addressed by management."
(is + being + addressed)

**Situation 2:** Present perfect passive (completed, current relevance)
Score 400-500: "The report has completed."
Score 650+: "The report has been completed."
(has + been + completed)

**Situation 3:** Past perfect passive (completed before past time)
Score 400-500: "By the time we arrived, the decision made."
Score 650+: "By the time we arrived, the decision had been made."
(had + been + made)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "is + past participle" for ongoing (needs "being") ✗
• Forgetting "been" in perfect passive ✗
• Mixing up continuous and perfect forms ✗

**Use:**
• Present continuous passive: is/are + BEING + past participle ✓
• Past continuous passive: was/were + BEING + past participle ✓
• Present perfect passive: has/have + BEEN + past participle ✓
• Past perfect passive: had + BEEN + past participle ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform: "They are reviewing the policy now. The policy already changed three times this year." → "The policy is being reviewed now. The policy has been changed three times this year."

## Assessment 64
**Assessment number:** 64
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** By-Agent (When to Include or Omit "By")

**Brief meaning:**
• **by someone** (when agent is important) - "designed by our team"
• **no "by"** (when agent is unknown/obvious/unimportant) - "The meeting was scheduled." (by whom doesn't matter)

**Pre-listening:** "In passive voice, we can include the agent (who did it) with 'by,' or omit it if it's unknown, obvious, or unimportant. TOEIC tests whether you know when to include or omit the by-agent."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Lower-scoring test-takers always include "by someone" or never do. Higher-scoring test-takers include it only when the agent is important or interesting information.

**Score 400-500:** "The report was written." (when the writer is important information)
**Score 650+:** "The report was written by the CEO." ✓ (including important agent)

Include "by" when the agent matters to the message.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Agent is important
Score 400-500: "The new policy was announced."
Score 650+: "The new policy was announced by the CEO."
(Who announced it is important → include "by CEO")

**Situation 2:** Agent is obvious/unimportant
Score 400-500: "The meeting was scheduled by someone."
Score 650+: "The meeting was scheduled for 2 PM."
(Who scheduled it doesn't matter → omit by-agent)

**Situation 3:** Agent is unknown
Score 400-500: "The window was broken by someone."
Score 650+: "The window was broken."
(Unknown who → omit by-agent)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Always including "by someone" ✗
• Including "by people" or "by someone" (vague) ✗
• Including agent when it's obvious ✗

**Use:**
• Include "by + agent" when agent is: important, interesting, surprising, or specific ✓
• Omit "by + agent" when agent is: unknown, obvious, unimportant, or "people in general" ✓
• Common omissions: "English is spoken worldwide" (by people, generally) ✓
• Common inclusions: "designed by Apple," "written by Shakespeare" ✓

**Recording Task:**
Decide on by-agent: "The report ___ (prepare) by the team. The decision ___ (make) yesterday." → "The report was prepared by the team" (team is relevant) vs. "The decision was made yesterday" (who made it is not specified/not important).

## Assessment 65
**Assessment number:** 65
**Sub-assessment type:** TOEIC 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Active vs. Passive Choice (When to Use Each)

**Brief meaning:**
• **Active voice** (focus on doer) - "The manager approved the budget."
• **Passive voice** (focus on receiver) - "The budget was approved."
• **Use passive when:** doer unknown, unimportant, or obvious; or when focusing on the action/result

**Pre-listening:** "Choose active voice when the doer is important. Choose passive voice when the action or result is more important than who did it. In business writing, passive is often used for diplomacy, objectivity, and formality."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Test-takers scoring 400-500 use active for everything or passive randomly. Those scoring 650+ choose based on focus: Who matters? → active. What was done matters? → passive.

**Score 400-500:** Not recognizing when passive is more appropriate
**Score 650+:** "Mistakes were made" ✓ (passive avoids blame) vs. "I made mistakes" (active accepts responsibility)

Passive can be more diplomatic and objective.

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Focus on action/result (passive better)
Score 400-500: "Someone completed the project on time."
Score 650+: "The project was completed on time."
(Focus on project completion, not who did it)

**Situation 2:** Focus on doer (active better)
Score 400-500: "The presentation was delivered by Sarah."
Score 650+: "Sarah delivered an excellent presentation."
(Focus on Sarah's performance → active is better)

**Situation 3:** Diplomatic/formal tone (passive better)
Score 400-500: "You didn't submit the report."
Score 650+: "The report was not submitted."
(Less accusatory, more diplomatic)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using passive voice everywhere ✗
• Never using passive voice ✗
• Not considering focus and tone ✗

**Use:**
• ACTIVE: when doer is important, known, and relevant ✓
• PASSIVE: when action/result is focus, doer is unknown/unimportant/obvious ✓
• PASSIVE: for diplomacy, objectivity, formality ✓
• Ask: "What's the main focus of this sentence?" → choose accordingly ✓

**Recording Task:**
Choose active or passive: "The CEO ___ (announce) major changes. Several positions ___ (eliminate)." → "The CEO announced major changes. Several positions were eliminated." (Active for CEO's action; passive for positions—more diplomatic)
