## Song 1: "They Match, We Match"
**Target Challenge 51: Subject-Verb Agreement Errors**

**Lexical Chunks:**
- **the company has** (singular)
- **the companies have** (plural)
- **each employee needs**
- **all employees need**
- **one of them is**
- **several of them are**
- **neither option seems**
- **both options seem**

---

**[Verse 1]**
**The company has** announced its quarterly results,
not "have announced," that's where confusion occurs,
**the companies have** merged to form something new,
singular takes "has," plural takes "have," it's true

**Each employee needs** to submit their form by Friday,
**all employees need** to attend, no one can hide away,
**one of them is** qualified for the promotion,
**several of them are** ready for that motion

**[Chorus]**
They match, we match, subjects and verbs align,
singular with singular, plural with plural, every time,
**the team is** working hard, **the teams are** in the race,
**the manager has** decided, **the managers have** their place

**[Verse 2]**
**Neither option seems** appropriate for the task at hand,
**both options seem** viable, help me understand,
the list of requirements **is** posted on the wall,
not "are" posted—"list" is singular, after all

**The data shows** improvement across the board,
wait—"data" can be plural, "**the data show**" is the word,
in formal writing, grammar, let precision be your guide,
**the company is**, **the companies are**, keep them side by side

**[Bridge]**
When phrases come between them, don't let your focus slip,
the subject's what determines, don't let the verb just flip,
"The box of documents **is**" not "are" on the shelf,
"boxes" is just extra, the subject is "box" itself

**[Final Chorus]**
They match, we match, subjects and verbs combine,
**everyone has** questions, **many have** concerns in line,
**the report is** ready, **the reports are** here to read,
subject-verb agreement is the grammar that we need

## Song 2: "Time Markers Tell the Tale"
**Target Challenge 52: Verb Tense Confusion**

**Lexical Chunks:**
- **yesterday we completed**
- **tomorrow we will complete**
- **currently we are completing**
- **since 2020 we have completed**
- **by next month we will have completed**
- **last year the sales increased**
- **every day the team works**
- **right now they are working**

---

**[Verse 1]**
**Yesterday we completed** the project ahead of schedule,
not "complete" or "will complete"—the past is what is factual,
**tomorrow we will complete** the second phase, that's clear,
**currently we are completing** tasks as deadlines near

**[Chorus]**
Time markers tell the tale of when the action took place,
**since 2020 we have completed** projects at this pace,
**last year the sales increased** by fifteen percent,
past tense for finished time, that's what the marker meant

**[Verse 2]**
**By next month we will have completed** all required training,
future perfect shows the timing, no need for more explaining,
**every day the team works** from nine to five o'clock,
present simple for routines, habits that never stop

**Right now they are working** on the quarterly review,
present continuous action, happening as we speak to you,
**since** and **for** need present perfect to convey,
**we have worked** together **for** ten years to this day

**[Bridge]**
**Before** the meeting started, **we had prepared** the slides,
past perfect shows the sequence, which action came before which tries,
**when** I arrived, the presentation **had already begun**,
**by the time** you read this, the contest **will have** been won

**[Final Verse]**
Time markers are your guide: **yesterday, tomorrow, now**,
**since, for, already, yet, still**—they tell you when and how,
**we finished**, **we will finish**, **we are finishing** the task,
match the tense to context, that's all the grammar asks

## Song 3: "Preposition Partners"
**Target Challenge 53: Preposition Choice Mistakes**

**Lexical Chunks:**
- **interested in** (not "interested to")
- **depend on** (not "depend of")
- **responsible for** (not "responsible of")
- **apologize for** (not "apologize about")
- **familiar with** (not "familiar to")
- **complain about** (not "complain for")
- **agree with** (someone) / **agree on** (something)
- **arrive at** (a place) / **arrive in** (a city)

---

**[Verse 1]**
I'm **interested in** the position that was advertised,
not "interested to"—that's wrong, let me get you advised,
we **depend on** accurate data for every decision we make,
not "depend of," that's a common mistake

**[Chorus]**
Preposition partners, they go hand in hand,
**responsible for** the project, help me understand,
you can't say "responsible of," that doesn't sound right,
**apologize for** the delay, make the grammar tight

**[Verse 2]**
Are you **familiar with** the software that we use?
not "familiar to"—wrong preposition, you'll confuse,
customers often **complain about** the service that they receive,
not "complain for," that's not the phrase we believe

**[Bridge - Two Distinctions]**
**Agree with** a person when you share their view,
**agree on** a topic, a plan, a decision—that's the clue,
you **arrive at** a building, a specific place,
**arrive in** a city, a country, a larger space

**[Verse 3]**
We **concentrate on** solving problems every day,
**differ from** each other in the approach we display,
**succeed in** our mission, **participate in** the event,
**account for** the expenses—that's what the preposition meant

**[Final Chorus]**
Preposition partners, learn them as a pair,
**apply for** a job, **apply to** a school—both are there,
**consist of** components, **result in** a gain,
prepositions make the meaning, they're part of the terrain

**[Outro - Spoken rapidly]**
Rely on, believe in, listen to, look for,
wait for, search for, care about, and more,
learn them as chunks, not words in isolation,
preposition partners—perfect combination!

## Song 4: "A, An, The—Or Nothing at All"
**Target Challenge 54: Article Usage Errors**

**Lexical Chunks:**
- **a meeting** (first mention, singular countable)
- **the meeting** (specific, already mentioned)
- **an employee** (vowel sound)
- **the employee** (specific person)
- **meetings are important** (plural general—no article)
- **information is valuable** (uncountable—no article)
- **the information you requested** (specific)
- **a university** (sounds like "yu")

---

**[Verse 1]**
We need to schedule **a meeting** for next Tuesday afternoon,
first mention, one of many, **the** would be too soon,
then later in the email: "**The meeting** will be at three,"
now it's specific, defined—**the** is the article we need

**[Chorus]**
**A, an, the**—or nothing at all,
**an employee** called today, did you take the call?
**the employee** who called is waiting in the hall,
**a, an, the**—or nothing at all

**[Verse 2]**
**An** before a vowel sound: **an hour**, **an opportunity**,
but "**a** university"—it sounds like "yu," you see,
**meetings are important** for communication flow,
no article with plural when you're speaking general, though

**[Bridge]**
**Information is valuable** in business every day,
uncountable nouns take no article when general is what you say,
but **the information you requested** is attached below,
specific now, use **the**—that's how the articles go

**[Verse 3]**
**The** when it's unique: **the CEO**, **the budget**, **the deadline**,
**a** when it's one of many: **a solution**, **a design**,
no article with abstracts: **success requires hard work**,
**patience is a virtue**, but **the patience he showed** lurked

**[Final Chorus]**
**A, an, the**—or nothing at all,
count or can't count, specific or general call,
**a proposal**, **the proposal**, **proposals** (none at all),
**a, an, the**—learn the rule, stand tall

**[Outro - Decision Tree Spoken]**
First mention? **A/an**. Mentioned before? **The**.
Specific? **The**. General plural? None for free.
Uncountable general? No article needed.
Specific uncountable? Add **the**—that's how it's treated.

## Song 5: "How It Happens—That's the Adverb"
**Target Challenge 55: Confusing Adjective vs. Adverb**

**Lexical Chunks:**
- **works efficiently** (adverb modifies verb)
- **an efficient worker** (adjective modifies noun)
- **speaks clearly** (adverb)
- **a clear explanation** (adjective)
- **carefully reviewed** (adverb before past participle)
- **a careful review** (adjective)
- **extremely important** (adverb modifies adjective)
- **seems easy** / **does it easily** (adjective after "seem," adverb after action verb)

---

**[Verse 1]**
She **works efficiently** on every task assigned,
not "efficient"—that's adjective, the adverb's what you'll find,
she is **an efficient worker**, that's the adjective way,
describing the noun "worker," not the verb "work" today

**[Chorus]**
How it happens—that's the adverb, add **-ly** to the end,
he **speaks clearly**, not "clear," let grammar be your friend,
**a clear explanation** uses the adjective form,
adjective for nouns, adverb tells how it's performed

**[Verse 2]**
We **carefully reviewed** the contract before signing,
adverb before the verb, that's the word we're finding,
it was **a careful review**, adjective describes the thing,
nouns need adjectives, verbs need adverbs—that's what they bring

**[Bridge - The Tricky Ones]**
After "be" or "seem" or "appear," adjectives are used,
the task **seems easy**, not "easily"—don't get confused,
but when it's an action: she **does it easily**,
adverb for the doing, adjective for what we see

**[Verse 3]**
This is **extremely important**—adverb modifying adjective,
"extremely" tells us how much, that's its objective,
he answered **correctly**, she responded **promptly** too,
**rapid growth**, but **growing rapidly**—choose the form that's true

**[Final Chorus]**
How it happens—that's the adverb, tells the manner, speed, or way,
adjectives describe the nouns, that's their role to play,
**quick** vs. **quickly**, **good** vs. **well**,
know your adjectives and adverbs, let the grammar gel

**[Outro - Common Pairs]**
**Good worker → works well**
**Bad performance → performed badly**
**Quick response → responded quickly**
**Successful project → succeeded successfully**
How it happens—adverb!
What it is—adjective!
