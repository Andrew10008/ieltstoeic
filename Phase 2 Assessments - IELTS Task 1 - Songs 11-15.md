## Song 3A: "More and Less" (Basic Comparisons)

---

## Assessment 26
**Assessment number:** 26
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Basic Comparative Structures - "higher/lower than"

**Brief meaning:**
• **higher than**
• **lower than**
• **more than**
• **fewer than**
• **less than**

**Pre-listening:** "IELTS Task 1 requires comparing data, not just describing it. Band 7+ writers use comparative structures to show relationships between different values or categories."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers describe data separately: "A was 50. B was 30." Band 7+ writers compare directly: "A was higher than B" or "A exceeded B by 20."

**Band 5-6:** "Sales in Asia were $500 million. Sales in Europe were $300 million."
**Band 7+:** "Sales in Asia were significantly higher than those in Europe, at $500 million compared to $300 million."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Comparing two values
Band 5-6: "Urban population was 70%. Rural population was 30%."
Band 7+: "Urban population was more than twice as high as rural population, at 70% compared to 30%."

**Situation 2:** Multiple comparisons
Band 5-6: "Country A produced 100 units. Country B produced 80 units. Country C produced 60 units."
Band 7+: "Country A's production was higher than Country B's, which in turn exceeded Country C's output."

**Situation 3:** Using "fewer" vs "less"
Band 5-6: "There were less people in 2020."
Band 7+: "There were fewer participants in 2020" (fewer = countable; less = uncountable)

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Describing each value separately without comparing ✗
• Using "less" for countable nouns (use "fewer") ✗
• Missing obvious comparison opportunities ✗

**Use:**
• "Higher/lower than" for values and percentages ✓
• "More/fewer than" for countable items ✓
• "Less than" for uncountable quantities ✓
• Include the actual figures after comparisons ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform these separate statements into comparisons: "Exports in 2010 were $200B. Exports in 2020 were $450B." → "Exports in 2020 were significantly higher than in 2010, more than doubling from $200B to $450B."

## Assessment 27

**Brief meaning:**
• **double the amount**
• **triple the amount**
• **twice as much as**
• **three times as many as**
• **half the size of**

**Usage Guide:**

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Doubling

**Situation 2:** Tripling
Band 5-6: "Renewable energy was 10% in 2000. It was 30% in 2020."
Band 7+: "Renewable energy's share tripled over the period, rising from 10% to 30%."

**Situation 3:** Fractions
Band 5-6: "Group A was 80. Group B was 40."
Band 7+: "Group B was half the size of Group A, at 40 compared to 80 participants."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Always stating separate numbers without showing relationship ✗
• Using "two times" (say "twice" or "double") ✗
• Missing multiplication patterns in the data ✗

**Use:**
• "Double/twice as much" for 2× relationships ✓
• "Triple/three times as much" for 3× relationships ✓
• "Half/one-third" for fractional relationships ✓

**Recording Task:**

## Assessment 28
**Assessment number:** 28
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Dominance - "outnumbered by"

**Brief meaning:**
• **outnumbered by**
• **exceeded by**
• **surpassed by**
• **outpaced by**

**Pre-listening:** "These passive structures are useful when the smaller/lower value is the grammatical subject of the sentence. They add variety to your comparative structures."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers use only active comparisons: "A was bigger than B." Band 7+ writers vary with passive: "B was outnumbered by A."

**Band 5-6:** "Female students were more than male students."
**Band 7+:** "Male students were outnumbered by female students, who comprised 65% of enrollment."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Quantity comparison
Band 5-6: "There were more imports than exports."
Band 7+: "Exports were exceeded by imports throughout the period, resulting in a persistent trade deficit."

**Situation 2:** Performance comparison
Band 5-6: "Renewable energy grew faster than fossil fuels."
Band 7+: "Fossil fuel growth was outpaced by renewable energy expansion, which increased at three times the rate."

**Situation 3:** Size comparison
Band 5-6: "The economy of China was bigger than Japan's."
Band 7+: "Japan's economy was surpassed by China's in 2010, when China became the world's second-largest economy."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using only active comparisons throughout ✗
• Overusing passive structures (balance is key) ✗
• Confusing "outnumbered" (for countable) with "exceeded" (for amounts) ✗

**Use:**
• "Outnumbered by" for countable items (people, cars, etc.) ✓
• "Exceeded by" for amounts, values, percentages ✓
• Vary between active and passive comparisons ✓

**Recording Task:**
Rewrite using passive comparison: "Asia's population growth exceeded Europe's by a wide margin." → "Europe's population growth was far exceeded by Asia's, which increased at nearly ten times the rate."

## Assessment 29
**Assessment number:** 29
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Describing Differences - "a significant difference"

**Brief meaning:**
• **a significant difference**
• **a considerable difference**
• **a marked difference**
• **a substantial difference**
• **a marginal difference**

**Pre-listening:** "Describing the SIZE of differences shows analytical skill. Band 7+ writers don't just compare - they indicate whether differences are large, small, or moderate."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers say "there was a difference." Band 7+ writers qualify it: "a significant difference," "a marginal difference."

**Band 5-6:** "There was a difference between men's and women's salaries."
**Band 7+:** "There was a considerable difference between men's and women's salaries, with men earning approximately 20% more."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Large difference
Band 5-6: "Urban and rural internet access were different."
Band 7+: "There was a marked difference in internet access between urban areas (95%) and rural regions (45%)."

**Situation 2:** Small difference
Band 5-6: "The two groups were a little different."
Band 7+: "There was only a marginal difference between the two age groups, varying by less than 3 percentage points."

**Situation 3:** Moderate difference
Band 5-6: "Performance varied between regions."
Band 7+: "A substantial difference in performance was evident between regions, with the north outperforming the south by 15%."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Just saying "different" without qualifying the size ✗
• Using the same qualifier repeatedly ✗
• Choosing qualifiers that don't match the data ✗

**Use:**
• "Significant/considerable/marked" for large differences ✓
• "Marginal/slight/modest" for small differences ✓
• "Substantial/notable" for moderate differences ✓
• Support with actual figures ✓

**Recording Task:**
Add appropriate qualifiers: "There was a difference in graduation rates. Urban schools had 92%, rural schools had 78%." → "There was a considerable difference in graduation rates, with urban schools achieving 92% compared to 78% in rural areas."

## Assessment 30
**Assessment number:** 30
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** The Gap Between - "the gap between X and Y"

**Brief meaning:**
• **the gap between X and Y**
• **the difference between X and Y**
• **the disparity between X and Y**
• **the divide between X and Y**

**Pre-listening:** "The noun phrase 'the gap between' is particularly useful in Task 1 for describing differences that widen, narrow, or remain constant over time."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers describe differences statically. Band 7+ writers track how gaps change: "the gap widened," "the gap narrowed."

**Band 5-6:** "Rich countries and poor countries were different in 2000. They were still different in 2020."
**Band 7+:** "The gap between rich and poor nations widened throughout the period, expanding from $20,000 to $35,000 per capita."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Widening gap
Band 5-6: "The difference got bigger."
Band 7+: "The gap between urban and rural incomes widened significantly, increasing from 30% to 45% over the two decades."

**Situation 2:** Narrowing gap
Band 5-6: "Male and female employment became more similar."
Band 7+: "The gap between male and female employment rates narrowed considerably, decreasing from 20 percentage points to just 5."

**Situation 3:** Persistent gap
Band 5-6: "The difference stayed the same."
Band 7+: "Despite economic growth, the disparity between the highest and lowest income groups remained relatively constant at approximately 10:1."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Saying "the difference" repeatedly (vary with gap/disparity/divide) ✗
• Missing opportunities to show how gaps change over time ✗
• Using "gap" for similarities (it implies difference) ✗

**Use:**
• "The gap widened/narrowed/remained constant" ✓
• "The disparity between" for formal emphasis ✓
• Quantify the gap with specific figures ✓

**Recording Task:**
Improve this description: "In 2000, Group A had 60% and Group B had 40%. In 2020, Group A had 75% and Group B had 35%." → "The gap between Group A and Group B widened over the period, expanding from 20 to 40 percentage points."

## Assessment 31
**Assessment number:** 31
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Divergence - "diverged from"

**Brief meaning:**
• **diverged from**
• **deviated from**
• **moved apart from**
• **separated from**

**Pre-listening:** "These verbs describe when two trends that were similar become different. They're particularly useful for describing data that starts together then moves in different directions."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers describe the end state: "they were different." Band 7+ writers describe the process of separation: "they diverged."

**Band 5-6:** "At first both countries had similar GDP growth. Later they were different."
**Band 7+:** "Initially both countries showed similar GDP growth, but their paths diverged after 2010, with Country A accelerating while Country B stagnated."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Describing separation
Band 5-6: "The two lines were together, then they went different ways."
Band 7+: "The two trend lines tracked closely until 2015, when they diverged sharply, moving in opposite directions."

**Situation 2:** Deviation from norm
Band 5-6: "One region was different from the national average."
Band 7+: "The northern region's unemployment rate deviated significantly from the national average, remaining consistently higher by 3-4 percentage points."

**Situation 3:** Increasing separation
Band 5-6: "Technology and manufacturing sectors became more different over time."
Band 7+: "Technology sector wages increasingly diverged from manufacturing wages, with the gap widening from 15% to 60% over two decades."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Missing the narrative of separation over time ✗
• Using "diverged" for data that was always different ✗
• Confusing "diverged" (became different) with "converged" (became similar) ✗

**Use:**
• "Diverged from" when similar things become different ✓
• Specify when the divergence began ✓
• Describe the nature of the divergence (opposite directions? different speeds?) ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform this into a divergence description: "In 2000, both cities had 5% unemployment. By 2020, City A had 3%, City B had 9%." → "Although both cities started with similar unemployment rates of 5% in 2000, their paths diverged significantly, with City A improving to 3% while City B's rate rose to 9%."

## Assessment 32
**Assessment number:** 32
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Basic Similarity - "similar to/comparable with"

**Brief meaning:**
• **similar to**
• **comparable with**
• **comparable to**
• **resembled**
• **was similar to**

**Pre-listening:** "Identifying similarities is as important as identifying differences in Task 1. Band 7+ writers note when data sets show similar patterns or values."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers focus only on differences. Band 7+ writers also note similarities: "A was similar to B," "both countries showed comparable growth."

**Band 5-6:** "Country A increased. Country B increased too."
**Band 7+:** "Country A's growth was similar to Country B's, with both nations experiencing comparable increases of around 3% annually."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Similar values
Band 5-6: "Both had about the same percentage."
Band 7+: "The figures were remarkably similar, with urban areas at 67% and suburban areas at 65%, a difference of just 2 percentage points."

**Situation 2:** Similar patterns
Band 5-6: "Both graphs went up then down."
Band 7+: "Both sectors displayed comparable patterns, rising steadily until 2015 before declining in subsequent years."

**Situation 3:** Resemblance
Band 5-6: "2020 looked like 2000."
Band 7+: "The 2020 distribution closely resembled that of 2000, with nearly identical proportions across all categories."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Overlooking similarities while focusing only on differences ✗
• Using "same" (use "similar" unless truly identical) ✗
• Saying "both are the same" (grammatically awkward) ✗

**Use:**
• "Similar to" for likeness ✓
• "Comparable with/to" for things that can be compared ✓
• "Resembled" for overall patterns ✓
• Note similarities even if there are small differences ✓

**Recording Task:**
Transform this into a similarity statement: "Exports from Region A were $450M. Exports from Region B were $440M." → "Export values from the two regions were remarkably similar, at $450M and $440M respectively, differing by less than 3%."

## Assessment 35
**Assessment number:** 35
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Both...and / Neither...nor

**Brief meaning:**
• **both X and Y**
• **neither X nor Y**
• **not only X but also Y**
• **as well as**

**Pre-listening:** "These paired structures help you describe similarities efficiently. Band 7+ writers use them to combine information about multiple data sets showing the same pattern."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers use separate sentences: "X increased. Y increased." Band 7+ writers combine: "Both X and Y increased."

**Band 5-6:** "Urban areas saw growth. Suburban areas saw growth too."
**Band 7+:** "Both urban and suburban areas experienced growth, with increases of 25% and 20% respectively."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Positive similarity
Band 5-6: "Men's participation increased. Women's participation increased."
Band 7+: "Both men's and women's participation rates increased, though women's growth was more substantial, rising by 15% compared to 8%."

**Situation 2:** Negative similarity
Band 5-6: "Coal didn't increase. Oil didn't increase."
Band 7+: "Neither coal nor oil consumption increased during the period, with both fossil fuels showing declining trends."

**Situation 3:** Not only...but also
Band 5-6: "Profits increased. Market share increased."
Band 7+: "The company saw growth not only in profits but also in market share, indicating strong overall performance."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Always describing items separately ✗
• Using "both" for more than two items (use "all") ✗
• Forgetting verb agreement: "both...increase" not "both...increases" ✗

**Use:**
• "Both X and Y" for two items showing same pattern ✓
• "Neither X nor Y" for two items NOT showing something ✓
• "Not only...but also" for emphasis ✓
• Can still note differences after establishing similarity ✓

**Recording Task:**
Combine these sentences: "Online sales increased by 40%. Mobile sales increased by 45%. Desktop sales decreased by 15%." → "Both online and mobile sales increased substantially, by 40% and 45% respectively, while desktop sales declined by 15%."

## Assessment 36
**Assessment number:** 36
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Beginning a Process - "first/initially/to begin with"

**Brief meaning:**
• **first**
• **initially**
• **to begin with**
• **at the first stage**
• **at the outset**

**Pre-listening:** "Process diagrams require sequential language. Band 7+ writers use varied expressions for the first stage instead of repeating 'first' every time."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers use only "first." Band 7+ writers vary: initially, to begin with, at the first stage, at the outset.

**Band 5-6:** "First, raw materials are collected. First, they are inspected."
**Band 7+:** "Initially, raw materials are collected. At the first stage, they undergo inspection."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Starting a manufacturing process
Band 5-6: "First, materials arrive at the factory."
Band 7+: "To begin with, raw materials are delivered to the factory and sorted by type."

**Situation 2:** Natural process opening
Band 5-6: "First, rainwater falls."
Band 7+: "Initially, rainwater is collected in catchment areas before entering the purification system."

**Situation 3:** Formal process description
Band 5-6: "First, applications are received."
Band 7+: "At the outset, applications are received and registered in the central database."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Using "firstly" (too informal; use "first" or "initially") ✗
• Starting multiple sentences with "first" ✗
• Forgetting to vary your opening expressions ✗

**Use:**
• "First/Initially" at the very beginning ✓
• "To begin with" for formal tone ✓
• "At the first stage" when describing staged processes ✓
• Follow with clear verb (often passive in processes) ✓

**Recording Task:**
Vary the opening: "First, cocoa beans are harvested. First, they are dried in the sun." → "Initially, cocoa beans are harvested from the trees. At the first stage, they are laid out to dry in the sun."

## Assessment 37
**Assessment number:** 37
**Sub-assessment type:** IELTS 1-50
**Discourse marker:** Sequential Steps - "then/next/after that/subsequently"

**Brief meaning:**
• **then**
• **next**
• **after that**
• **subsequently**
• **following this**

**Pre-listening:** "After the first step, you need to guide readers through the sequence. Band 7+ writers use varied connectors instead of repeating 'then' for every step."

**Usage Guide:**
**MEANING FOCUS:**
Band 5-6 writers overuse "then." Band 7+ writers vary: next, after that, subsequently, following this.

**Band 5-6:** "Then it is washed. Then it is cut. Then it is packaged."
**Band 7+:** "Next, it is thoroughly washed. After that, it is cut into portions. Finally, it is packaged for distribution."

**Practice Scenarios:**

**Situation 1:** Multi-step sequence
Band 5-6: "Then the mixture is heated. Then it is cooled. Then it is molded."
Band 7+: "Next, the mixture is heated to 200°C. Subsequently, it is cooled rapidly. Following this, it is molded into the desired shape."

**Situation 2:** Manufacturing process
Band 5-6: "Then components are assembled. Then they are tested."
Band 7+: "After initial inspection, components are assembled by trained technicians. The completed units are then tested for quality assurance."

**Situation 3:** Natural cycle
Band 5-6: "Then water evaporates. Then it forms clouds."
Band 7+: "Next, water from oceans and lakes evaporates due to solar heat. Subsequently, this vapor rises and forms clouds in the atmosphere."

**Recording Tips:**

**Avoid:**
• Starting every sentence with "then" ✗
• Using "and then" excessively ✗
• Losing track of the sequence ✗

**Use:**
• "Next" for the immediate following step ✓
• "After that/following this" for formal tone ✓
• "Subsequently" for academic writing ✓
• Vary throughout the description ✓

**Recording Task:**
Improve this repetitive sequence: "Then grapes are crushed. Then juice is fermented. Then wine is aged." → "Next, the grapes are crushed to extract the juice. After that, the juice undergoes fermentation for several weeks. Subsequently, the wine is aged in oak barrels."

## Assessment 38

• **once**
• **when**

**Use:**
• "Once" to emphasize completion ✓

• "After" + noun phrase or clause ✓
• "As soon as" for immediate subsequent action ✓
