# Comprehensive Guide to Common TOEIC Errors (400-600 Score Range)

This guide provides a detailed breakdown of common grammatical errors, vocabulary issues, and test-specific challenges faced by TOEIC test-takers in the 400-600 score range.

## 1. Subject-Verb Agreement

**The Basic Rule:** Singular subjects take singular verbs, and plural subjects take plural verbs.

**Common Errors:**

- **Separated Subject and Verb:** Students often get confused when the subject and verb are separated by a phrase or clause. They might incorrectly make the verb agree with the noun closest to it, rather than the actual subject.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "The list of required documents _are_ on the desk."
    - Example (Correct): "The list of required documents _is_ on the desk."
    - Example (Incorrect): "The box of chocolates and flowers _were_ delivered today."
    - Example (Correct): "The box of chocolates and flowers _was_ delivered today."
- **Compound Subjects:** When two singular subjects are joined by "and," they usually take a plural verb. However, there can be exceptions (when the two nouns form a single unit of thought).
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "My colleague and I _is_ going to the conference."
    - Example (Correct): "My colleague and I _are_ going to the conference."
    - Example (Correct - Single Unit): "Ham and eggs _is_ my favorite breakfast."
    - Example (Incorrect): "Bread and butter _is_ all we need."
    - Example (Correct): "Bread and butter _are_ sold at the grocery"
- **Indefinite Pronouns:** Some indefinite pronouns (like "each," "every," "someone," "anyone," "everybody," "nobody," "nothing") are singular and require singular verbs, while others (like "some," "all," "none," "most") can be singular or plural depending on the context (what they refer to).
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "Everyone _have_ received their login credentials."
    - Example (Correct): "Everyone _has_ received their login credentials."
    - Example (Incorrect): "Each of the candidates _have_ their own strengths."
    - Example (Correct): "Each of the candidates _has_ their own strengths."
    - Example (Correct - Plural): "Some of the _employees are_ unhappy."
    - Example (Correct - Singular): "Some of the _information is_ incorrect."
- **Collective Nouns:** Nouns that refer to a group (team, committee, staff, company) can be singular or plural depending on whether the group is acting as a unit or as individuals.
    
    - Example (Singular - Unit): "The committee _is_ meeting on Tuesday."
    - Example (Plural - Individuals): "The committee _are_ arguing among themselves."

## 2. Misusing Countable and Uncountable Nouns

- **Countable Nouns:** These can be counted and have singular and plural forms (e.g., book, books; chair, chairs). They can be used with articles "a/an" in the singular.
- **Uncountable Nouns:** These cannot typically be counted and usually do not have a plural form (e.g., information, advice, furniture, money, research, progress, traffic, weather, work). They are generally not used with "a/an."

**Common Errors:**

- **Pluralizing Uncountable Nouns:** Adding an "s" to uncountable nouns.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "We need _informations_ about the new project."
    - Example (Correct): "We need _information_ about the new project."
    - Example (Incorrect): "He gave me several good _advices_."
    - Example (Correct): "He gave me several good _pieces of advice_."
- **Using "a/an" with Uncountable Nouns:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "Could you give me _an advice_?"
    - Example (Correct): "Could you give me _some advice_?" or "Could you give me _a piece of advice_?"
- **Using "many" or a number with Uncountable Nouns:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "There are _three furnitures_ in the room."
    - Example (Correct): "There are _three pieces of furniture_ in the room."
    - Example (Incorrect): "I have _many homeworks_ tonight."
    - Example (Correct): "I have _much homework_ tonight" or "I have _many assignments_ tonight."
- **Using "fewer/less" Incorrectly:**
    
    - **"Fewer" is for countable nouns.**
    - **"Less" is for uncountable nouns.**
    - Example (Incorrect): "There are _less_ people at the beach today."
    - Example (Correct): "There are _fewer_ people at the beach today."
    - Example (Incorrect): "I have _fewer_ money than I thought."
    - Example (Correct): "I have _less_ money than I thought."

## 3. Confusing Prepositions

Prepositions indicate relationships between words, often related to time, place, or direction (e.g., in, at, on, for, since, to, from, by, with, about, of).

**Common Errors:**

- **Prepositions of Time:** Incorrectly using "in," "at," or "on" with specific times, days, or months.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "The meeting is _in_ Monday."
    - Example (Correct): "The meeting is _on_ Monday."
    - Example (Incorrect): "I'll see you _on_ the afternoon."
    - Example (Correct): "I'll see you _in_ the afternoon." (Use "on" for specific days, "in" for parts of the day, "at" for specific times).
    - Example (Correct): "The event is _at_ 3:00 PM."
    - Example (Incorrect) "The deadline is _at_ March."
    - Example (Correct): "The deadline is _in_ March."
- **Prepositions of Place:** Mixing up "in," "at," or "on" when referring to locations.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I work _in_ the airport."
    - Example (Correct): "I work _at_ the airport." (Use "at" for specific locations/points, "in" for enclosed spaces, "on" for surfaces).
    - Example (Incorrect): "The keys are _in_ the table."
    - Example (Correct): "The keys are _on_ the table."
    - Example (Incorrect): "The office is located _on_ London."
    - Example (Correct):"The office is located _in_ London."
- **Prepositions with Verbs (Phrasal Verbs and Verb + Preposition Combinations):** Using the wrong preposition after certain verbs.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I'm looking forward _to_ see you."
    - Example (Correct): "I'm looking forward _to seeing_ you." (Gerund after "to" in this specific case).
    - Example (Incorrect): "He apologized _about_ his mistake."
    - Example (Correct): "He apologized _for_ his mistake."
    - Example (Incorrect): "We are relying _to_ your help."
    - Example (Correct): "We are relying _on_ your help."
    - Example (Incorrect): "They are interested _on_ the project."
    - Example (Correct): "They are interested _in_ the project."

## 4. Confusing Verb Tenses

Verb tenses indicate when an action took place (past, present, future).

**Common Errors:**

- **Incorrect Simple Past vs. Present Perfect:** Misunderstanding when to use the simple past (for completed actions at a specific time in the past) and the present perfect (for actions that started in the past and have a connection to the present, or for actions within a time period that is not yet finished).
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I _have visited_ Paris last year."
    - Example (Correct): "I _visited_ Paris last year." (Specific, finished time).
    - Example (Incorrect): "I _went_ to that restaurant many times since I moved here."
    - Example (Correct): "I _have been_ to that restaurant many times since I moved here." (Continuing period).
    - Example (Incorrect): "She _has finished_ the report yesterday."
    - Example (Correct): "She _finished_ the report yesterday."
    - Example (Correct - Present Perfect): "She _has finished_ the report." (No specific past time mentioned, implies recent completion).
- **Inconsistent Tenses:** Not maintaining a consistent verb tense within a sentence or paragraph.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "She _arrived_ late and then _starts_ to complain."
    - Example (Correct): "She _arrived_ late and then _started_ to complain."
    - Example (Incorrect): "I _go_ to the store yesterday and _bought_ some milk."
    - Example (Correct): "I _went_ to the store yesterday and _bought_ some milk."
- **Future Tense Confusion:** Incorrectly using "will" vs. "going to" or the present continuous for future plans. "Will" is often for spontaneous decisions, predictions, or promises. "Going to" is often for pre-planned intentions. The present continuous is for _definite_ arrangements.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I _will_ go to the gym tomorrow." (If it's a definite plan).
    - Example (Correct - Definite Plan): "I _am going_ to the gym tomorrow." or "I _am going to go_ to the gym tomorrow."
    - Example (Correct - Spontaneous Decision): "I'm thirsty. I _will_ get a drink."
    - Example (Incorrect): "Next week, I _will buy_ a new car." (If it's already planned).
    - Example (Correct):"Next week, I _am going to buy_ a new car."

## 5. Using the Wrong Comparatives and Superlatives

- **Comparatives:** Used to compare _two_ things (e.g., better, more efficient, easier).
- **Superlatives:** Used to compare _three or more_ things (e.g., best, most efficient, easiest).

**Common Errors:**

- **Using Comparative Instead of Superlative:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "This is the _better_ solution of all the options."
    - Example (Correct): "This is the _best_ solution of all the options."
- **Using Superlative Instead of Comparative:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "Between the two candidates, John is the _most qualified_."
    - Example (Correct): "Between the two candidates, John is the _more qualified_."
- **Incorrect Formation:** Not following the rules for forming comparatives and superlatives (adding "-er/-est" for short adjectives, using "more/most" for longer ones).
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "This task is _more easier_ than the last one."
    - Example (Correct): "This task is _easier_ than the last one."
    - Example (Incorrect): "He is the _most smartest_ person I know."
    - Example (Correct): "He is the _smartest_ person I know."
    - Example (Incorrect):"This phone is _expensiver_."
    - Example (Correct): "This phone is _more expensive_."
- **Double Comparatives/Superlatives**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "This is _more better_ than that."
    - Example (Correct): "This is _better_ than that."

## 6. Article Usage (a, an, the)

Articles specify whether a noun is general or specific.

**Common Errors:**

- **Missing Articles:** Forgetting to use "a/an" with singular countable nouns when they are first mentioned or when they represent a general example of something.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I need to buy pen."
    - Example (Correct): "I need to buy _a_ pen."
    - Example (Incorrect): "He is doctor."
    - Example (Correct): "He is _a_ doctor."
- **Incorrect Use of "the":** Using "the" when the noun is not specific or has not been mentioned before (unless it's something unique, like "the sun").
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I went to _the_ store yesterday." (If the listener doesn't know which store, and it hasn't been mentioned before).
    - Example (Correct): "I went to _a_ store yesterday." or "I went to _the_ store near my house yesterday."
    - Example (Correct - Unique): "The sun is shining brightly."
- **Unnecessary Articles:** Using articles with uncountable nouns or plural nouns when they are referring to something in general.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I like _the_ coffee." (If referring to coffee in general).
    - Example (Correct): "I like coffee."
    - Example (Incorrect): "The happiness is important."
    - Example (Correct): "Happiness is important."
    - Example (Correct - Specific): "The coffee in this cafe is excellent." (Specific type of coffee).
    - Example (Incorrect): "_The_ books are useful for learning." (General statement).
    - Example (Correct): "Books are useful for learning."

## 7. Pronoun Agreement

Pronouns must agree in number (singular/plural) and gender (male/female/neuter) with the nouns they refer to (antecedents).

**Common Errors:**

- **Number Disagreement:** Using a singular pronoun to refer to a plural noun, or vice versa.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "The team members are working hard on _his_ project."
    - Example (Correct): "The team members are working hard on _their_ project."
    - Example (Incorrect): "Each student must bring _their_ own lunch."
    - Example (Correct): "Each student must bring _his or her_ own lunch." (Or, better, rephrase to avoid the awkwardness: "All students must bring their own lunches.")
- **Gender Disagreement:** Using a pronoun of the wrong gender. (Less common in business contexts, but still possible).
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "The CEO presented _her_ report, and he was very pleased." (If the CEO is male).
    - Example (Correct): "The company announced profits that exceeded _her_ projections." (If 'her' refers to something other than the company.)
    - Example (Correct): "The CEO presented _his_ report, and he was very pleased."
- **Vague Pronoun Reference:** It should be clear which noun a pronoun refers to.
    
    - Example (Vague): "The manager told the employee that he needed to work harder." (Who needs to work harder?)
    - Example (Clear): "The manager told the employee, 'You need to work harder.'"

## 8. Punctuation Errors

Correct punctuation is essential for clear and understandable writing.

**Common Errors:**

- **Comma Splices:** Joining two independent clauses (complete sentences) with only a comma.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "The meeting was productive, we discussed several important issues."
    - Example (Correct): "The meeting was productive; we discussed several important issues." or "The meeting was productive, and we discussed several important issues." or "The meeting was productive. We discussed several important issues."
- **Run-on Sentences:** Joining two or more independent clauses without any punctuation or conjunction.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "The project was delayed the team faced unexpected challenges."
    - Example (Correct): "The project was delayed because the team faced unexpected challenges."
- **Fragmented Sentences:** Incomplete sentences that do not express a complete thought.
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "Although the report was late."
    - Example (Correct): "Although the report was late, it was very comprehensive."
- **Incorrect Use of Apostrophes:** Mistakes with possessives (e.g., "the company's profits" vs. "the companies profits") and contractions (e.g., "it's" vs. "its").
    
    - Example (Incorrect - Possessive): "The companies profits were impressive."
    - Example (Correct - Possessive): "The company's profits were impressive."
    - Example (Incorrect - Contraction): "Its a good idea."
    - Example (Correct - Contraction): "It's a good idea." (It is).
    - Example (Incorrect - Possessive): "The dog wagged it's tail."
    - Example (Correct - Possessive): "The dog wagged its tail."

## 9. Sentence Structure Issues

- **Word Order:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "Yesterday went I to the market."
    - Example (Correct): "I went to the market yesterday."
- **Parallelism:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect):"He enjoys swimming, to hike, and riding his bike."
    - Example (Correct): "He enjoys swimming, hiking, and riding his bike."

## 10. Passive vs. Active Voice

- Example (Incorrect - unnecessary passive): "The report was written by the team."
- Example (Correct - active): "The team wrote the report."
- Example (Incorrect - wrong passive form): "The record company has troubled by the Scandal."
- Example (Correct - passive): "The community has been plagued by drug addiction."

## 11. Conjunctions and Linking Words

- Example (Incorrect): "He was tired, because he went to bed early."
- Example (Correct): "He was tired, _so_ he went to bed early."

## 12. Relative Pronouns (who, whom, which, that, whose)

- Example (Incorrect): "The person _which_ is speaking is the manager."
- Example (Correct): "The person _who_ is speaking is the manager."
- Example (Incorrect): "The man _who_ I spoke to him was very helpful." (Redundant pronoun)
- Example (Correct): "The man _whom_ I spoke to was very helpful." (Or, more commonly in spoken English: "The man I spoke to...")
- Example (Incorrect): "The book _who_ is on the table is mine."
- Example (Correct): "The book _that_ is on the table is mine." ("That" or "which" for things).

## 13. Time-Related Conjunctions (since, for, while, during, until)

- Example (Incorrect): "I have lived here _during_ five years."
- Example (Correct): "I have lived here _for_ five years." or "I have lived here _since_ 2020."

## 14. Modal Verb Confusion

- **Incorrect Modal Combinations:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "You _must to_ finish the report by Friday."
    - Example (Correct): "You _must_ finish the report by Friday."
    - Example (Incorrect): "I _can to_ speak English."
    - Example (Correct): "I _can_ speak English"
- **Confusing Modals of Obligation:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I _have to can_ speak English for this job." (Mixing obligation and ability).
    - Example (Correct): "I _have to_ speak English for this job." (Obligation) or "I _can_ speak English." (Ability)
- **Misusing Modals for Probability:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "It _can_ rain tomorrow." (When discussing probability, not possibility in general).
    - Example (Correct): "It _might/may/could_ rain tomorrow."
    - Example (Incorrect) "He _must_ be American because he speaks good English." (must implies certainty).
    - Example (Correct): "He _might_ be American because he speaks good English."

## 15. Passive Voice Errors (Beyond Basic Form)

- **Incorrect Formation:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "The report writing by the team."
    - Example (Correct): "The report _was written_ by the team."
- **Unnecessary Use of Agent:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "The form was completed _by someone_."
    - Example (Correct): "The form was completed."
- **Tense Confusion in Passive Voice:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "The meeting _is held_ yesterday."
    - Example (Correct): "The meeting _was held_ yesterday."
    - Example (Incorrect):"The documents _will be send_ tomorrow."
    - Example (Correct): "The documents _will be sent_ tomorrow."

## 16. Conditional Sentence Structure

- **Mixed Conditionals:** (Using incorrect combinations of tenses)
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "If I _will have_ time, I _would_ attend the conference."
    - Example (Correct): "If I _have_ time, I _will_ attend the conference."
- **Incorrect Verb Forms:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "If they had arrived earlier, they _will see_ the presentation."
    - Example (Correct): "If they had arrived earlier, they _would have seen_ the presentation."
    - Example (Incorrect): "If we _would study_ harder, we would pass the test."
    - Example (Correct): "If we _studied_ harder, we would pass the test."
- **Omitting "Would" in the Result Clause:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "If I won the lottery, I _buy_ a house."
    - Example (Correct): "If I won the lottery, I _would buy_ a house."

## 17. Gerunds and Infinitives

- **Incorrect Verb Patterns:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I enjoy _to read_ business magazines."
    - Example (Correct): "I enjoy _reading_ business magazines."
    - Example (Incorrect): "She stopped _smoking_ to say hello." (When meaning changed activity)
    - Example (Correct): "She stopped _to smoke_." (Purpose) or "She stopped _smoking_." (Ceased the habit)
- **Preposition + Gerund:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I'm interested in _attend_ the conference."
    - Example (Correct): "I'm interested in _attending_ the conference."
- **Forget/Remember/Try + Infinitive/Gerund:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I forgot _buying_ the tickets." (When intent was to express forgetting a past action)
    - Example (Correct): "I forgot _to buy_ the tickets." (Failed to remember to do something)
    - Example (Correct): "I forgot _buying_ the tickets." (Don't remember the act of buying)

## 18. Reported Speech/Indirect Speech

- **Tense Shifts:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "She said she _is_ coming to the meeting."
    - Example (Correct): "She said she _was_ coming to the meeting."
- **Question Word Order:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "He asked _where was_ the conference room."
    - Example (Correct): "He asked _where the conference room was_."
- **Modal Verb Changes:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "The manager said we _will_ need to work overtime."
    - Example (Correct): "The manager said we _would_ need to work overtime."

## 19. Quantifiers with Countable/Uncountable Nouns

- **Misusing "Much" and "Many":**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "There are _much_ people waiting."
    - Example (Correct): "There are _many_ people waiting."
    - Example (Incorrect): "How _many_ progress have you made?"
    - Example (Correct): "How _much_ progress have you made?"
- **Incorrect Combinations:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "I don't have _many_ time."
    - Example (Correct): "I don't have _much_ time."
    - Example (Incorrect): "There is _a few_ water left."
    - Example (Correct): "There is _a little_ water left."

## 20. Listening-Specific TOEIC Errors

- **Missing Contractions and Reduced Forms:**
    
    - Example: Not recognizing "I'd've" as "I would have" or "could've" as "could have"
    - Example: Missing "wanna," "gonna," "gotta" for "want to," "going to," "got to"
- **Missing Linking Words in Rapid Speech:**
    
    - Example: Not hearing the connection in "Did you eat yet?" → "Dijeet yet?"
    - Example: Missing "Would you like to" → "Wouldja like ta"
- **Confusing Similar-Sounding Words:**
    
    - Example: Missing the distinction between "affect/effect," "accept/except"
    - Example: Confusing numbers like "fifteen/fifty," "fourteen/forty"

## 21. TOEIC-Specific Reading Challenges

- **Misunderstanding Reference Words:**
    
    - Example: Not identifying what "this," "that," "these," "those," "it," "they" refer to in longer passages
- **Missing Implied Information:**
    
    - Example: Not inferring that "The office closes at 5 PM" means you can't enter after 5 PM
- **Overlooking Signal Words:**
    
    - Example: Missing transitions like "despite," "although," "nevertheless" that indicate contrasting ideas
    - Example: Not recognizing sequence markers like "firstly," "subsequently," "finally"

## 22. Common Business English Collocations

- **Incorrect Verb-Noun Combinations:**
    - Example (Incorrect): "Let's _do_ a decision about this."
    - Example (Correct): "Let's _make_ a decision about this."
    - Example (Incorrect): "Our company _did_ significant progress."
    - Example (Correct): "Our company _made_ significant progress."
- **Prepositional Collocations in Business:**
    - Example (Incorrect): "The increase _of_ sales"
    - Example (Correct): "The increase _in_ sales"
    - Example (Incorrect): "We're responsible _of_ customer service."
    - Example (Correct): "We're responsible _for_ customer service."

## 23. Word Order Problems

- **Misplaced Adverbs:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "She finishes always her work on time."
    - Example (Correct): "She always finishes her work on time."
- **Question Formation:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "Why you are leaving early today?"
    - Example (Correct): "Why are you leaving early today?"
- **Adjective Order:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "She bought a leather expensive black bag."
    - Example (Correct): "She bought an expensive black leather bag."

## 24. Vocabulary Limitations

- **Overreliance on General Words:**
    
    - Example (Vague): "The company had a good thing happen to their business."
    - Example (Better): "The company experienced significant growth in the third quarter."
- **Incorrect Collocations:**
    
    - Example (Incorrect): "Do a mistake"
    - Example (Correct): "Make a mistake"
- **Confusing Words with Similar Meanings:**
    
    - Example: Using "job" when "position" or "role" would be more appropriate in a formal context

## 25. Test-Taking Strategy Issues

- **Failure to Skim and Scan:**
    
    - Not previewing questions before listening or reading
- **Second-Guessing Correct Answers:**
    
    - Changing initial correct responses due to uncertainty
- **Literal Translation from Native Language:**
    
    - Constructing responses based on first language structure rather than English
- **Time Management Problems:**
    
    - Spending too much time on difficult questions
    - Not leaving enough time to check answers
