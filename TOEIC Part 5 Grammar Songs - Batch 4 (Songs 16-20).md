## Song 16: "Read the Whole Story"
**Target Challenge 66: Ignoring Sentence Context**

**Lexical Chunks:**
- **based on the context**
- **surrounding words provide clues**
- **the sentence before** / **the sentence after**
- **what makes sense here**
- **eliminate answers that don't fit**
- **consider the meaning, not just grammar**

---

**[Verse 1]**
You see four words that all look grammatically right,
but only one makes sense when you check with insight,
**based on the context** of what the sentence conveys,
**surrounding words provide clues** in subtle ways

**[Chorus]**
Read the whole story, not just the blank,
**the sentence before** and **the sentence after**—thank
the context for showing what word belongs,
grammar alone isn't enough for these songs

**[Verse 2]**
"The project was _____ on schedule."
Is it "completed," "delayed," "based," or "depended"?
All could work grammatically in this position here,
but "completed" or "delayed" make the meaning clear

**[Bridge - Strategy]**
**What makes sense here** given what they're trying to say?
**eliminate answers that don't fit** the logical way,
"depended on" needs "on," so that doesn't work,
"based on" needs context that doesn't lurk

**[Verse 3 - Real Example]**
"Due to budget constraints, we must _____ the project."
(A) expand (B) postpone (C) celebrate (D) recommend
Context clue: "budget constraints" = problems, you see,
(B) postpone makes sense—that's the key!

**[Final Chorus]**
Read the whole story, absorb the full meaning,
**consider the meaning, not just grammar** gleaming,
context determines which answer is right,
read surrounding sentences to bring truth to light!

## Song 17: "Yesterday, Today, Tomorrow"
**Target Challenge 67: Missing Time Markers**

**Lexical Chunks:**
- **yesterday** → past tense
- **last week/month/year** → past tense
- **tomorrow** / **next week** → future (will)
- **currently** / **now** / **at present** → present continuous
- **since** / **for** → present perfect
- **by the time** → future perfect or past perfect
- **every day** / **usually** → present simple

---

**[Verse 1]**
**Yesterday** we _____ (complete) the annual report,
time marker signals past—**completed**, that's the sort,
**last week** the company _____ (announce) new policies,
past tense required—**announced** with ease

**[Chorus]**
Yesterday, today, tomorrow—time markers show the way,
**currently** we _____ (work) → **are working**, they say,
**every day** she _____ (review) → **reviews** the mail,
time markers tell the tense, without fail!

**[Verse 2]**
**Tomorrow** we _____ (present) the findings to the board,
future signal clear—**will present** is the word,
**next month** the team _____ (launch) the new campaign,
**will launch** with time marker—future's the domain

**[Bridge - Present Perfect Markers]**
**Since** 2020, we _____ (expand) into new markets,
**have expanded**—present perfect targets,
**for** ten years, she _____ (work) in this department,
**has worked**—ongoing to present moment

**[Verse 3 - Compound Markers]**
**By the time** you arrive, I _____ (finish) the presentation,
**will have finished**—future perfect formation,
**when** I arrived, the meeting _____ (already/start),
**had already started**—past perfect, smart!

**[Final Chorus]**
Time markers are your guide to choosing tense,
**currently**, **yesterday**, **tomorrow**—it makes sense,
**usually**, **always**, **often** → present simple habit,
**now**, **at present** → continuous—grab it!

**[Outro - Quick Reference]**
PAST: yesterday, last..., ago, in 2020
PRESENT: now, currently, at present, these days
FUTURE: tomorrow, next..., soon, in 2030
PERFECT: since, for, already, yet, just, recently
Read the markers—choose correctly!

## Song 18: "Does It Fit the Sentence?"
**Target Challenge 68: Not Checking Answer Fit**

**Lexical Chunks:**
- **grammatically correct**
- **logically sound**
- **fits the meaning**
- **makes sense in context**
- **reads naturally**
- **check before and after**
- **must agree with surrounding text**

---

**[Verse 1]**
It might be **grammatically correct** standing alone,
but does it fit this sentence? Check the tone,
is it **logically sound** with what comes before and after?
does it **fit the meaning** or cause disaster?

**[Chorus]**
Does it fit the sentence, does it make sense here?
**check before and after**, make it crystal clear,
grammar's just the first step, meaning is the test,
**must agree with surrounding text** to be the best

**[Verse 2 - Example]**
"The proposal was _____ by the committee."
(A) rejected (B) happy (C) expensive (D) quickly
(B) and (C) don't work with "was" + past participle form,
(D) "quickly" is adverb, doesn't fit the norm,
(A) "rejected" is passive voice, **fits the meaning** clear,
grammatically and logically—that's what we hear!

**[Bridge - The Double Check]**
Read your answer back into the sentence whole,
does it **read naturally**? That's the goal,
"The meeting will held tomorrow"—something's wrong,
"will BE held" is needed to make it strong

**[Verse 3 - Agreement Check]**
If the subject is plural, does your verb agree?
If it's uncountable, no "a" or "many" we should see,
**fits the meaning** of increase or decrease?
check the logic—grammatical, at least!

**[Final Chorus]**
Does it fit? Is it right? Check it twice,
grammatically correct AND **logically sound** advice,
meaning and grammar must both align,
then you know your answer is fine!

## Song 19: "Count the Subject, Match the Verb"
**Target Challenge 69: Overlooking Subject Plurality**

**Lexical Chunks:**
- **the report is** (singular)
- **the reports are** (plural)
- **information is** (uncountable - singular)
- **each... is** (singular)
- **all... are** (plural)
- **one of the employees is** (singular)
- **several employees are** (plural)
- **the team is** (collective - usually singular)

---

**[Verse 1]**
**The report is** ready for your review,
singular subject—singular verb, that's true,
**the reports are** due by the end of the week,
plural subject—plural verb, that's what we speak

**[Chorus]**
Count the subject, match the verb, make them agree,
singular or plural? Look carefully,
**information is** valuable (uncountable = singular),
**data is** or **are**? Both forms particular!

**[Verse 2]**
**Each employee is** required to attend the training,
**each** is always singular, no complaining,
**all employees are** invited to the celebration,
**all** is plural here in this situation

**[Bridge - Tricky Ones]**
**One of the employees is** responsible for this task,
"one" is the subject (singular)—that's what I ask,
**several employees are** working on the project together,
**several** is plural, always, no matter the weather

**[Verse 3 - Collectives]**
**The team is** preparing for the presentation,
collective nouns are usually singular in this nation (American English),
but "**the team are** arguing among themselves,"
when acting as individuals, plural delves (British English)

**[Verse 4 - Uncountables]**
**Equipment is** expensive to maintain and replace,
**furniture is** being delivered to the new office space,
**advice is** valuable when it comes from experts,
uncountable nouns—singular verbs, no deserts!

**[Final Chorus]**
Count the subject carefully, don't be fooled by noise,
**the list of items is** long, not "are"—singular voice,
**the items on the list are** expensive, they say,
now "items" is the subject—plural way!

**[Outro - Memory Aid]**
Find the REAL subject (ignore prepositional phrases),
count it: one or many? That's what amazes,
match the verb to the subject's number clear,
subject-verb agreement—precision here!

## Song 20: "Because, Therefore, Although"
**Target Challenge 70: Missing Logical Connectors**

**Lexical Chunks:**
- **because** / **since** / **as** (cause/reason)
- **therefore** / **thus** / **consequently** (result)
- **although** / **though** / **even though** (concession/contrast)
- **so that** / **in order to** (purpose)
- **whereas** / **while** (comparison/contrast)
- **if** / **unless** (condition)
- **when** / **while** / **as** (time)

---

**[Verse 1 - Cause and Result]**
We must leave early **because** traffic will be heavy,
**because** introduces the reason, steady,
traffic will be heavy; **therefore**, we must leave early,
**therefore** shows the result, pearly

**[Chorus]**
**Because**, **therefore**, **although**—
logical connectors make relationships show,
cause, result, contrast, or condition set,
choose the right connector—logical net!

**[Verse 2 - Contrast and Concession]**
**Although** sales decreased, profits remained stable,
**although** shows contrast—unexpected, but able,
sales decreased; **however**, profits remained stable,
semicolon before **however**—that's the table

**[Bridge - Purpose]**
We hired more staff **so that** we could meet demand,
**so that** shows purpose—that was the plan,
we hired more staff **in order to** meet demand,
infinitive of purpose—understand

**[Verse 3 - Condition and Comparison]**
**If** we receive approval, we will proceed with the plan,
**if** introduces condition—contingent span,
**unless** we receive approval, we cannot proceed,
**unless** means "if not"—that's what we need

**Whereas** the first quarter showed growth, the second showed decline,
**whereas** contrasts two situations in line,
**while** I appreciate your effort, I cannot approve this request,
**while** shows concession—gentle protest

**[Final Chorus - Complete Set]**
CAUSE: **because**, **since**, **as**, **due to** (+ noun)
RESULT: **therefore**, **thus**, **so**, **consequently** soon
CONTRAST: **although**, **however**, **whereas**, **while**
PURPOSE: **so that**, **in order to**, compile
CONDITION: **if**, **unless**, **provided that**
TIME: **when**, **while**, **as**, **after** that!

**[Outro - Usage Rule]**
Coordinating conjunctions: **and**, **but**, **or**, **so**
can join sentences with comma—let it flow,
subordinating: **because**, **although**, **if**, **when**
start dependent clauses—remember them!
